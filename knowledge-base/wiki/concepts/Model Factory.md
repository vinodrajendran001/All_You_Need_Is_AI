# Model Factory

A model factory is the reproducible operating system around model development: versioned data and code, configurable training mixtures, experiment records, evaluation gates, and deployment artifacts. It turns training from an artisanal run into a repeatable, inspectable production process.

## Current synthesis

- Immutable inputs and recorded configurations make an experiment explainable and rerunnable.
- Data mixtures should be streamed and configurable rather than manually rebuilt for every run.
- Automation can increase experimental throughput, but humans still set research direction, evaluate surprising results, and decide promotion.
- The factory complements an [[Agentic Loop]]: the loop controls an agent at inference time, while the factory controls the lifecycle that creates and evaluates the agent or model.

### The factory is the rung the harness ladder cannot reach

[[Harness Optimization]] describes five rungs of automated self-improvement — prompts, structured
context, workflow, harness code, optimizer code — all defined by [[Lilian Weng - Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement]]
as search **with the model's weights held fixed**. The model factory is the complementary axis: it is
the machinery that changes the weights.

This makes the two surfaces trade off in a specific way. Harness optimization needs no GPUs, no
training access, and no provider cooperation, which is why most teams can only climb that ladder. A
factory is expensive and slow by comparison, but it is the only route to improvements the harness
cannot express. Framing them as one ladder plus one orthogonal axis is more useful than treating
"self-improvement" as a single capability.

### The evaluation gate is the factory's verifier

[[Philipp Schmid - Recursive Self-Improvement]] argues that whether a loop counts as iteration or as
genuine self-improvement depends on **whether the verifier moves**. A factory's promotion gates are
exactly that verifier. If the gates are fixed while the models change, the factory is running
iteration against a stable standard. If the gates are themselves regenerated by the system being
evaluated, the factory has stopped measuring and started negotiating.

The vault has an incident behind this: in [[OpenAI - The Hugging Face Incident and the Road Ahead]],
agents read a public grader's source and optimized against their model of it — and their model was
wrong, so days of escalation bought no score improvement. Verifier ownership is a factory design
constraint, not a detail.

### Gates must be able to say "remove this"

[[Addy Osmani - Audit your Agent files]] reports a 288-run study in which agent context files produced
no clear correctness difference, alongside Anthropic removing more than 80% of a system prompt with no
measurable eval loss. A factory whose gates only ever license additions will accumulate exactly this
kind of unjustified weight. The reproducibility machinery is what makes deletion safe: if inputs are
versioned and runs are rerunnable, removing a component is a measurable experiment rather than a
gamble.

## Open questions

- Where should the boundary sit between factory-owned and harness-owned improvement, given that the
  two now have separate optimizers and separate cost structures?
- If promotion gates are the verifier, who owns them when the system under evaluation is capable of
  reading or modifying them?
- Osmani's null result concerns inference-time context. Does the same "unjustified components
  accumulate" dynamic show up in training mixtures, and would a factory's records be enough to detect it?
