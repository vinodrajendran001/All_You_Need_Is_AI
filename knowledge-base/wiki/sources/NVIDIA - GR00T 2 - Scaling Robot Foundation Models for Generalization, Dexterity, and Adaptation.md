# GR00T 2: Scaling Robot Foundation Models for Generalization, Dexterity, and Adaptation

## Summary

NVIDIA's GEAR lab research page introduces GR00T 2, a family of "world-action models" (WAMs). One
diffusion transformer jointly denoises future video and robot actions, and small per-robot heads
turn the plan into motor commands. Pretraining mixes about **402,730 hours** of human egocentric
video, **10,184 hours** of handheld-gripper demonstrations, and **23,500 hours** of robot
teleoperation. The page reports transfer from humans and handheld devices to robots that never
practiced a task, few-shot adaptation, and real-robot reinforcement learning (RL). It also reports
control by a reasoning agent and visual steering with grasp and release marks. A smaller variant,
GR00T 2 Turbo, runs on a Unitree G1 humanoid. Simulation is used for training data and as an
evaluator that tracks real-world rankings. Every result is first-party and based on modest trial
counts. Many comparisons appear only in charts, and those values are not reported here.

## Key claims

### One model predicts the scene and the action

GR00T 2 reads recent video, an instruction, and the robot state. It denoises future video and an
action chunk together, executes the chunk, and repeats. The page's claim is that predicting how the
scene should change "lets GR00T 2 learn physics from human video". Its example rollout runs on
Dexter, a Tianji robot with Sharpa hands, at the model's own **320×224** resolution from **0.8 s**
of real history. The future frames shown are generated by the model, not real footage.

### The pretraining mixture is mostly human video

- **402,730 h** of egocentric video of people at work, from NVIDIA's Human-1M dataset;
  **100,000 h** are open on Hugging Face as Human-1M-100K.
- **10,184 h** of handheld UMI-gripper demonstrations across **2,897** tasks.
- **23,500 h** of teleoperation: YAM **15,146 h**, ABC-130k on YAM **3,591 h**, Sharpa hands
  **2,890 h**, Wuji hands on Tianji arms **1,256 h**, DROID (Franka) **350 h**, and Unitree G1
  **275 h**. These components sum to 23,508 h (derived), consistent with the rounded total.

Four models were pretrained on none, **1%**, **10%**, and **100%** of the human video, then
evaluated on **72** downstream Dexter tasks. Task progress rises at every step, from **64% to 77%**
in video rollouts and from **50% to 70%** on the real robot. Error bars are one standard deviation
across repeated runs.

### X-Adapt: demonstrations need not come from the target robot

- **Human → Dexter.** Four chemistry-lab tasks were demonstrated only by gloved people wearing
  head and wrist cameras. Task-agnostic play data teach Dexter its own body. Over **20** trials per
  task, GR00T 2 "succeeds on all four tasks" with zero teleoperated demonstrations.
- **UMI → YAM.** Conveyor sorting, a toy dump truck, and NIST board disassembly were demonstrated
  only with a handheld gripper. On the conveyor, GR00T 2 sorts **86%** of **50** items, against
  **28%** for GR00T 1.7 and **0%** for π0.5.
- **DexUMI → hand.** A wearable exoskeleton matching a robot hand's joints teaches three bimanual
  tasks on a Tianji robot with XHAND1 hands, including striking a match to light a lamp.

### Adaptation is cheap, and dexterity needs little task data

On **50** tasks seen only in human video or other robots' data, GR00T 2 succeeds in **40%** of
trials zero-shot, and **40 of 50** tasks succeed at least once. Most misses are said to be precision
failures in a single step, not wrong plans. One demonstration per task lifts success to **71%**.
Five demonstrations, **under 3 minutes** of robot data, lift it to **95%**, with **37 of 50** tasks
succeeding in every trial. Each adaptation is a **30-minute** LoRA fine-tune on a single RTX 5090.
On Dexter's two **22-DoF** Sharpa hands, it seats GB300 memory modules (**85%**), assembles a toy
truck (**65%**), and makes a shipping label (**60%**, against **5%** for GR00T 1.7 and **0%** for
π0.5). A hammer skill went from one human demonstration through simulation and RL to a real robot;
no success rate is given.

### Real-world RL and an agent push contact-rich work further

RL on the real robot, "driven only by task-level success signals", lifts three contact-rich tasks
from **40–76%** after supervised post-training to **88–96%** with "GRPO-induced self-imitation". On
fixed-pipe fittings the reported rates are **88%** for that method, **86%** for GRPO, **76%** for
residual RL, and **66%** for SFT.

A frontier reasoning agent, run as "System 2", decides *what* to do; GR00T 2 decides *how*. In an
observe–reason–act–verify loop, the agent calls GR00T 2 through a WAM API on an MCP tool server. The
same server offers camera, robot-state, and code-as-policy (CaP) tools, including recovery skills
learned with ASPIRE. Every assembly trial starts with the parts dumped on the table. The agent
setup completes the toy plane, tractor, and Hot Wheels car in **98, 97, and 97 of 100** trials, or
**292 of 300**. The post-trained policy without the agent reaches **71–84**, and supervised
post-training alone **23–69**. The agent also chains RL-trained insertions into tasks of five
insertions each. Replacing the supervised policy with the RL-trained one raises full-task success
from **12 to 19 of 20** runs for Ethernet cables and from **2 to 20 of 20** for chargers.

### Visual steering: marks on the camera view

A planner marks a grasp point and a release point on GR00T 2's camera view, coloured by the arm that
should act. GR00T 2 fills in the motion. The marks are extracted from gripper and hand states in
existing demonstrations, so "training needs no new data". For short what-and-where instructions,
success rises from **40% to 92%** on YAM and from **30% to 70%** on Dexter. The planner's step
instructions alone reach **66%** and **33%**. For instructions about *how* to move, the gain is
**44% to 59%** on YAM. Over long horizons on YAM, nothing completes without marks; with them,
trash sorting succeeds **17 of 20** times and board disassembly **3 of 10**. On Dexter, full tasks
are rarely completed.

### Turbo runs on the humanoid

GR00T 2 Turbo uses a **2B**-parameter transformer and a higher-compression video encoder. A
prediction takes **38 ms** on an RTX 5090 and **124 ms** on the G1's Jetson Thor, where it plans
**1.6 s** chunks about once per second. The SONIC whole-body controller executes them at **50 Hz**.
Shared inference optimizations cut median latency by **66–86%** (**2.9–7.2×**) relative to eager
BF16. On the G1, Turbo serves popcorn in all **48** trials and clears a table in **93%** of
trials, with **30** trials for each task other than popcorn. On YAM, it beats GR00T 2 on fixed-pipe
fittings (**90%**) and wire-into-breaker insertion (**100%**). It trails GR00T 2 on the other four
YAM tasks and on five of six Dexter tasks, tying on the sixth.

### Simulation adds data and serves as the evaluator

Adding simulated episodes raises Turbo's out-of-distribution G1 success from **6.7% to 63.3%**;
GR00T 1.7 rises to **40.0%**. An agentic real-to-sim workflow uses SimFoundry tools to build digital
twins from robot videos, 3D scans, and photographs. These are validated by automated tests and human
feedback. Across **14** GR00T 2 policies on a **25**-task YAM benchmark, simulated and real success
correlate at Pearson **0.92**, Spearman **0.89**, and MMRV **0.055**. Elo scores correlate at
Pearson **0.93** and Spearman **0.90**. Data-pipeline changes and inference settings were screened in
simulation before robot tests.

## Tensions / open questions

- This is a first-party research page, and every number comes from NVIDIA's own evaluations. Trial
  counts are modest, from **10** to **100** per task or setting. Apart from the human-video scaling
  chart's error bars, no uncertainty is reported.
- Many comparisons, including the cross-embodiment ablation, most per-task rates, and latency
  bars, appear only in inline charts. They are excluded here, so prose numbers are a subset.
- The baselines are the earlier GR00T 1.7 and π0.5. Their training budgets, data, and tuning are
  not stated.
- The **40%** zero-shot figure counts tasks seen in other embodiments' data or human video, so
  "unseen" means unseen on the target robot, not novel to the model.
- "GRPO-induced self-imitation" is named but not specified. Its difference from GRPO is **88%**
  against **86%** on one shown task.
- The capture does not say whether the RL fixed-pipe task (SFT **66%**) is the YAM post-training
  fixed-pipe task on which Turbo scores **90%**. The two figures should not be compared directly.
- The real-to-sim agreement covers one robot, one benchmark, and **14** policies from one family.
  MMRV is not defined in the capture.
- The agentic results depend on an unnamed frontier reasoning model and on CaP recovery skills;
  their individual contributions are not separated.
- There is no byline or publication date. A BibTeX block names "NVIDIA et al." as author of a 2026
  whitepaper, which was not captured. The ID date is the capture date.
