# 2026-09-13 Lint Pass

## Scope

Thirty-fourth comprehensive pass over **449 wiki pages** and **250 top-level raw captures**:

- 225 source summaries
- 110 concepts
- 69 entities
- 1 overview and 1 synthesis
- 33 pre-existing lint reports
- 243 source IDs at the start of the pass

The audit checked frontmatter, source ownership, three-way control parity, code-aware wikilinks,
wrapped links, index coverage, semantic orphans, `## Affected pages` reciprocity, self-links,
Windows-compatible filenames, tag namespaces, summary compression, and qualifier propagation.

## Findings

### Fixed in this pass

1. **One raw clipping was stored as a source summary.** A complete 3,827-word Google Cloud clipping
   had no source-summary frontmatter, summary structure, inbound links, or `## Related pages`.
   It was preserved in `knowledge-base/raw/inbox/` for a later formal ingest rather than treated as
   a malformed wiki page.
2. **Three thin summaries had compressed away the claim their caveat qualified.**
   [[@neural_avb - What Are Looped Transformers|What Are Looped Transformers?]] now records the
   fixed-compute disadvantage, sequential recurrence, sampled loop counts, and the newer Loopie
   counterexample. [[@waterloo_intern - From GPT-2 to Kimi K3]] now retains 2.8T, 124M, and the
   approximately 22,580x headline while distinguishing total capacity from active compute.
   [[Netflix - In-House LLM Serving]] no longer says Netflix deliberately reduced its metric surface:
   Triton's bridge exposed only 9 of more than 40 vLLM metrics, so Netflix restored the missing
   signals through a unified proxy.
3. **The compressed claims had propagated downstream.** The corrected recurrence evidence now
   appears in [[Recursive Architectures]] and [[Linear Attention and Recurrent Memory]]; the Kimi
   comparison now appears in [[Linear Attention and Recurrent Memory]] and [[Mixture of Experts]];
   and the Netflix mechanism is corrected in [[Inference Serving Engines]] and
   [[Serving Benchmarks and Goodput]].
4. **The defensive-deception concept was too thin for its evidence.**
   [[Defensive Deception for Open Models]] now preserves the seven-model/five-family scope, the six
   gate-passing models, the 0.51–0.90 decoy range, the +0.27 to +0.84 defense-attributable range,
   the failed Qwen3.5-9B boundary result, matched-quality and majority-vote checks, clean-state
   controls, first-release constraint, and governance risk. [[Mark Russinovich - Fool's Gold]] now
   carries the same decisive numbers and limitations.
5. **Four source-summary filenames contained private-use characters standing in for `?` and `:`.**
   These names were not portable across Git and Windows. The looped-Transformer page and the
   three-part Giles Thomas GPT-2 series now use portable filenames, with aliases preserving their
   readable titles and all inbound links updated.
6. **The control pages omitted eight real component source IDs.** The ByteByteGo May batch summary
   owns one aggregate ID and eight component IDs, but only the aggregate appeared in the controls.
   [[index]], [[log|Knowledge Base Log]], and [[AI Knowledge Base Overview]] now contain the same
   **251 IDs**, exactly equal to the source-summary ownership set. This aligns the ByteByteGo batch
   with the existing Alireza Diri aggregate-source policy.
7. **Eight legacy source summaries lacked `source_ids`.** Their ownership lists now explicitly carry
   their primary `source_id`; this closes all six raw IDs that appeared unowned under strict
   traceability checks.
8. **Two source summaries linked to themselves**, and two flashcard raw-capture links were ambiguous
   because an asset and its wrapper shared a basename. The self-links were removed and the four
   flashcard links now use explicit raw-source paths.
9. **One summary title was truncated.** [[Virinchi T - Google Cloud MCP Security Framework]] now
   preserves the raw source's complete title in `source_title`.

### Clean after repair

spans, not navigation. Likewise, the eight source IDs initially reported as "used but uncontrolled"
were the ByteByteGo aggregate's component captures, not stray concept citations.

## Fixes made

- Moved the misplaced clipping from `wiki/sources/` to `raw/inbox/`.
- Repaired three lossy summaries and propagated the corrected claims to five concept pages.
- Rebuilt one underdeveloped concept and strengthened its source summary.
- Normalized four non-portable filenames and their inbound links.
- Added eight missing component IDs to all three control pages.
- Added `source_ids` to eight legacy summaries.
- Removed two semantic self-links and path-qualified four ambiguous raw links.
- Corrected one truncated `source_title`.

## Follow-ups

- The Gemini Enterprise multi-tenant clipping in `raw/inbox/` is preserved but not yet ingested.
- **46 legacy raw captures** still predate explicit `source_id` metadata. Their summaries remain
  controlled and reachable, but strict raw-ID validation cannot inspect those captures without an
  explicit metadata-normalization pass.
- A heading-only heuristic flags 31 legacy summaries and 45 concepts that do not use every modern
  template heading. The pages are not uniformly thin or malformed, so bulk section insertion would
  create noise; future passes should continue semantic review source by source.
- Historical overlap between `## Affected pages` and `## Related pages` remains redundant on many
  summaries, but it does not break navigation or reciprocity and should not be bulk-edited without a
  schema decision.
