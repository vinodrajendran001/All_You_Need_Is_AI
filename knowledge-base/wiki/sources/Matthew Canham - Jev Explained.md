# Matthew Canham - Jev Explained

## Summary

Matthew Canham explains Jev as a generalized classifier that receives state, a typed question, and
possible answers, then returns a choice and probabilities rather than generated prose.

## Key claims

- Noul represents a binary probability, Choice supports up to **255 options**, and Score represents
  an ordinal scale of **2-10 levels**.
- The post claims **20-200x** faster and **40-400x** cheaper operation than comparable LLMs.
- Suggested uses include routing, fraud, recommendations, prioritization, and tutoring.

## Why it matters

The state-question-options framing is a concise interface model for separating decisions from
language generation.

## Tensions and caveats

This is an independent explainer, not a technical specification or evaluation. Its performance
ranges are unsourced, and it establishes neither architecture, calibration, nor correctness. The
vault retains [[Typed Probabilistic Decision Models]] rather than adopting the vendor category as neutral.
