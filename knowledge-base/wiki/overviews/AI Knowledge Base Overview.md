# AI Knowledge Base Overview

## Scope

This wiki is the persistent knowledge layer for the `All_You_Need_Is_AI` vault. It is meant to accumulate sources, syntheses, and durable answers about AI instead of rediscovering them from scratch on every query.

## Current picture

As of October 23, 2026, the wiki has **605 typed pages**, including **321 source summaries**
owning **347 source IDs**, **136 concepts**, and **88 entities**; page totals exclude git-ignored
local query files. The raw collection contains **349 top-level captures** and **418 files in
total**, not counting the four Obsidian settings files under `raw/sources/.obsidian/`;
recaptures and equivalent clippings remain preserved without being counted as independent
evidence.

The workspace now implements Karpathy's three-layer pattern:

- Raw sources live under `knowledge-base/raw/`.
- Curated wiki pages live under `knowledge-base/wiki/`.
- The operating schema lives in the root `CLAUDE.md` note.

**An anti-collapse term derived rather than tuned.**
[[Siddhant Rai - LeJEPA Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning]] is the batch's best piece of

predicts linear-probe accuracy at **~85% Spearman, ~99% rescaled** — label-free model selection. The

DINOv3**. New page: [[Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture]]. See [[Siddhant Rai]], [[Vizuara]].

## September 30 additions

**Knowing the future request is worth more than making the model faster.**
[[Modal - Hitting a Billion Tokens per Minute on One GPU]] hands the serving engine a SQL query plan,
which turns cache eviction from a heuristic into a schedule and makes prefetching exact.
[[Jacky Kwok et al - Contrastive Language Models]] reaches the same place from the model side: because
candidate action embeddings do not depend on the state, they can be computed once and reused, so a
decision over four cached candidate actions costs one forward pass instead of five in the paper's
Super Mario example. Neither result is about a better model. Both are about a request
structure that lets the expensive half be computed ahead of time — the same move
[[Jerry Liu - Just-in-Time Agentic OCR]] makes in retrieval, now visible in serving and in decisions.

**Interaction is the term the ranking heuristics throw away.**
[[Antonio Tiene et al - Pruning LLMs Like a Physicist]] shows that the best set of M transformer blocks
to delete is generally not the M individually least-important blocks: on Llama-3.3-70B without
retraining, removing 40 of 80 blocks holds MMLU at 76.9 under constrained binary optimization versus
54.0 under per-block influence ranking. [[Inferact - 700 TPS on Kimi K3 - A Case for TPU Megakernels]]
carries the same lesson into scheduling, where collapsing the decoder into one kernel wins small-batch
decode on hardware with lower peak FLOPS and lower HBM bandwidth than the GB200, measured per GPU,
that it is compared against. Both are vendor-reported, and both degrade in the regimes their authors
name — larger batches for the megakernel, lighter compression ratios for the pruner. The megakernel
comparison also runs a hand-written kernel against a published vLLM GB200 recipe, so kernel effort is
not controlled and the win cannot be attributed to the technique alone.

**Compute demand tracks deliberation per user, not users.**
[[FD - Agent Muse Compute Demand]] estimates that at a hypothetical 100M daily active users, the entire
agent sandbox layer would draw roughly 0.1 GW against a 1-2 GW total, with inference carrying the rest.
Every input is an assumption rather than a measurement, but the structural claim survives the
uncertainty: an agent that reasons more per task raises compute demand without acquiring a single new
user. That is the cost side of the same test-time-scaling trade the vault records on the quality side.

**Evaluation has to score the refusals.**
[[Lance Martin - Automating Eval Design and Hillclimbing with Claude]] supplies controls for
benchmark overfitting — a held-out split, one attributable patch per round, a revert rule, a noise
floor below which edits are not attempted, and a warning at roughly 95% that the metric is saturated
and the objective should change. [[ByteByteGo - Why Do LLMs Lie]] adds what the metric should contain:
correctness, support, appropriate abstention, and unnecessary refusal, because right-or-wrong scoring
cannot tell a system that learned to say "I don't know" from one that learned to stop answering.

**Context that nobody maintains is not context.**
[[Jina Yoon - We're Building Multiplayer AI]] reports that over 90 days, 64 users started a shared
context file and 14 ever edited it. PostHog's replacement derives context from work that already
carries a commitment — what shipped, merged, or was decided — rather than asking people to curate it.
It is an internal observation without a denominator, but it tests an assumption the vault's
context-engineering pages have largely taken on faith.

**Two new surfaces arrive with the same gap.**
[[ByteByteGo - AI Agents Can Think, Now They Can Pay]] describes inline HTTP-native payment where a
spending cap bounds how much an agent can lose but not what it buys, and
[[Sarthak Rastogi - 6 Ways to Use Jev to Make AI Agents More Reliable]] is explicit that a typed
screening model is a filter rather than a security boundary because it does not treat its input as
hostile. In both cases the new mechanism narrows blast radius without establishing intent or identity,
so least-privilege controls remain underneath.
[[Tyler Romero - Policy Gradient for LLMs, Explained Visually]] supplies the identity beneath the
vault's applied RL evidence: at binary verifier reward the gradient reduces to a log-likelihood on the
correct completions only, so failures are never pushed down directly, and a group whose completions
all succeed or all fail centres to zero and contributes no gradient. This is a pedagogical derivation,
not a measurement, and the SFT equivalence is a property of the gradient rather than a claim that RL
and SFT share training dynamics.

## October 19 additions

**Interpretability: is the network's own unit already legible?**
[[Nick Cammarata - Neural Networks Are Already Clean]] seeds [[Mechanistic Interpretability]]
and [[Goodfire]]. Goodfire argues that the canonical evidence for superposition was misread.
In its account, the cat-and-car neuron detects a V shape, and InceptionV1's messy last layer
reflects architecture and tooling failures. Most remaining polysemanticity, it says, is
"recursive generalization." Its Qwen3-32B figure of about 90% of neuron poles having one main

shows that removing weak activations is cheap in a final block but drops accuracy to 3.80% and

without merging them. Neither shows that superposition is absent or that internals give a

**A judge is only as good as the human reference behind it.**
[[Sri Polkampally et al - LLM-as-Judge When It Works and When It Misleads]] gives
[[LLM-as-a-Judge]] its first clinical exclusion zones. A validated human baseline comes first.
Tasks stay narrow (semantic equivalence or standardized reasoning). Conclusions are
population-level rather than about individual cases, and judge outputs never become ground
truth. The "slop cycle" and closed-API deprecation become grader failure modes on
[[Benchmark Optimization]] and [[Open Model Ecosystems]]. Vendor backend swaps undo
procurement-time validation on [[Embedded Evaluation]] and [[LLM Application Resilience]].
The source is an argued rationale for a public comment, with no agreement threshold or
statistics.

**Generation need not be token by token, but the comparison is against diffusion.**
[[Alpha Signal - How Next-Concept Modeling Rethinks AI Text Generation]] reports Stanford's
Next-Concept Modeling. It generates a coarse-to-fine hierarchy of discrete codes, one
sequential step per level. Its 10–31× speedups are against diffusion language models at
roughly 100M parameters, not against autoregressive decoding. The summarization gain comes
from spending that speed on more candidates. [[Diffusion Models]], [[LLM Inference]], and
[[Test-Time Scaling]] carry those boundaries. The clipping has no byline, date, or canonical
URL.

**Which parameters policy gradient can move is bounded before any reward is seen.**
[[Yingru Li - The Gradient Is a Covariance]] derives that, on-policy, the expected gradient is
the covariance of reward and score, capped by the Fisher. A prompt-level baseline changes only
variance, and off-policy updates need the score centered under a known sampler.
[[Group Relative Policy Optimization]], [[Reinforcement Learning]], and [[LLM Reasoning]] take
the derivation. The 5–30% sparsity and 47.40% overlap figures are secondhand citations, and a
bound on the expected gradient is not a bound on an optimizer's update.

**Infrastructure fails in degraded states, not only dead ones.**
[[Netflix - Reliable GPU Compute for Training and Inference]] seeds [[GPU Fleet Reliability]].
The page covers fail-closed health checks, graded remediation, attribution before retry, and
goodput objectives split by cause. Health is kept separate from AI21's admission and placement
layer. Netflix's >99.95% availability and ~98% to above 99.5% job success have no window or
denominators.
[[Sunita Nadampalli and Nicolò Lucchesi - Push-Based KV Transfer in vLLM]] overlaps decode-side
registration with prefill on [[Prefill-Decode Disaggregation]]. Its "up to 3x" TTFT gain is one
1P1D sweep's extreme cell, measured with prefix caching disabled. Push also gives up choosing
the decode instance after prefill, which pull keeps for load-aware routing.

**Robot data has to be manufactured, and hours are not a common currency.**
[[Brian Potter - How to Train Your Robot]] seeds [[Robot Training Data]]. Teleoperation,
deployment, simulation, human video, and UMI each trade among embodiment fidelity, physical
accuracy, diversity, and cost. Company-reported volumes and text-equivalence conversions agree

[[Rhoda AI - Scaling Web-Video Pre-training for Real Robots]] remains the vault's only
controlled transfer study.

[[ByteByteGo - EP229 Chunking Strategies for RAG-Based Agents]] defines eight strategies
without measurements. [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] and [[Embedding Model Selection]]
record which component each strategy hands the boundary to. [[Agent Skill]] records the

## October 23 additions

**One monitorability number hides which way it moved.**
[[Melody Guan et al - Complementary Metrics for Monitorability]] is OpenAI publishing a
critique of its own headline metric. Dataset-level g-mean² stays primary, but two complements
are added: pair-matched monitorability, which compares models only on instances both support,
and post-control risk, which asks what survives when the monitor becomes a rejection gate and
the model retries. [[Chain-of-Thought Monitoring]] now records that dataset-level
monitorability can fall while per-instance monitorability rises, and that a monitor wired to a
gate measures something different from one wired to a report.
[[Interpretability Evaluation]] takes the sampling point: more samples narrow sampling error
but do not identify an unobservable quantity. [[Embedded Evaluation]] takes the gate's
counterpart risk, that a low bad-output rate can mean the system rarely deployed anything. The
retry policy is left unspecified, which bounds how far post-control risk can be reproduced.
