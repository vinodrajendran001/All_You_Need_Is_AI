# Ethan Mollick - The Dot and the Swarm

## Summary

Mollick revises his expectation that humans must carefully design multi-agent organizations.
He argues that improving models may choose useful coordination structures themselves, leaving the
human as the source of goals and authority rather than the designer of every handoff. This is an
opinion and experience essay, not a benchmark of autonomous organizations.

## Key claims

- Personal-assistant examples show agents undertaking work and choosing to delegate, including
  examples involving **three versus thirteen agents**. They illustrate behavior, not controlled
  evidence that either agent count is more effective.
- Learned coordination may reduce the need for manually prescribed roles and workflows. It does
  not eliminate the need to decide what should be done, whose interests count, or what authority
  an agent receives.
- Mollick discusses both useful and unwanted coordination. The same ability to work together can
  amplify a task or an unauthorized goal; coordination competence is not alignment.
- His extraordinary Navier-Stokes account describes **88 hours and roughly 2.7 million messages**,
  while explicitly saying the claimed result is **not formally accepted**. This ingest does not
  verify the event or treat it as an established mathematical proof.
- He remains uncertain about whether these systems can sustain the long, unglamorous work of
  real organizations. Demonstrations of impressive tasks do not answer that operational question.

## Why it matters

The essay challenges the assumption that more human-written topology is always the path to mature
agents. Read alongside Replit's implementation, it suggests separating the choice of coordination
from the nondelegable responsibility to define goals, enforce permissions, and verify outcomes.

## Tensions / open questions

The anecdotes, incident reporting, and unreleased-model safety claims have different evidence
strengths and were not independently checked. The essay does not measure cost, failure frequency,
task selection, or the counterfactual performance of a single agent. Its prediction about less
scaffolding remains compatible with deterministic business gates and runtime isolation.
