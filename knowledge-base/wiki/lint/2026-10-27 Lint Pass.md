# 2026-10-27 Lint Pass

## Scope

Forty-fourth comprehensive pass, run after the October 27 ingest. The starting inventory was **625 typed
wiki pages**, counted from the working tree. Adding this report leaves **626**, including **335 source
summaries**, **136 concepts**, **92 entities**, and **44 lint reports**. Evidence ownership is **361 source
IDs**. The raw collection holds **365 top-level captures**.

Structural coverage was the standing audit: frontmatter schema and directory/type agreement, source
ownership and exact index/log/overview parity, link resolution and reachability, index categories and
uncited captures, Related-pages endings, heading structure, tags, and the source/post/private-query
boundaries. After the link restoration described below, it returned **zero findings**, and it returned
zero at the end.

Three checks ran for the first time:

- a **reciprocity check** across the whole vault in both directions, with a third rule for sources cited
  in body prose;
- a **quote check**, requiring every double-quoted passage added by a fix to occur verbatim in a raw
  capture;
- a **survey of raw-capture names and metadata** against the conventions in `CLAUDE.md`.

The semantic review used six parallel read-only passes, partitioned by subject: hardware and
accelerators; security and governance; durable execution and multi-tenancy; cost, caching, and routing;
evaluation, retrieval, and memory; and safety, judging, and reward design. They reported **59 findings**:
4 high, 36 medium, and 19 low severity. Each finding was checked against the raw capture before any page
was edited.

## Findings

### An outside commit rewrote one link in 29 files

On 2026-10-24 an outside auto-commit, `8deb40a`, changed exactly one Databricks link in each of 29 files,
from the bare entity name to the vault-absolute path
`All_You_Need_Is_AI/knowledge-base/wiki/entities/Databricks`. The 29 files were 11 concepts, 1 entity, 14
source summaries, and all three control pages, and the commit changed nothing else. The [[Databricks]]
entity was never renamed, and the vault holds only one Databricks note. The most likely cause is
Obsidian's automatic link updating, which is enabled at the vault root, reacting to a duplicate note that
existed briefly and was never committed.

Obsidian still resolved the rewritten links, because the path is relative to the parent vault. Checks that
resolve links from this repository could not: **14 of the 15** rule-B reciprocity violations below were
this artifact, not missing evidence. The October 27 ingest noticed the rewrite and left it for this pass.

All 29 links were restored to the bare form, as an exact inverse with line endings preserved. One restored
line is in [[log]], inside the October 23 ingest entry. That returns the entry to the text it was written
with; it does not rewrite history.

### Reciprocity, measured with a third rule

The October 23 pass checked two rules across the whole vault and cleared them:

- **A:** a summary lists a page under `## Affected pages`, but the page lacks the summary's ID.
- **B:** a page carries a source ID, but the owning summary does not list the page.

This pass added a third:

- **C:** a concept, entity, or synthesis page links a source summary in its body, above
  `## Related pages`, without carrying that summary's ID.

Measured on the pre-lint baseline, the counts were **A 0, B 15, C 16**. Fourteen of the B violations were
the Databricks artifact. The fifteenth was real: [[Salesforce]] carried the ID of
[[Sarthak Rastogi - Making AI Agents Observable, Monitorable, and Production-Ready]], whose affected list
did not include it.

Eleven of the C violations predated October 23. The other five came from the October 27 ingest: four on
the new [[Cloudflare]] entity and one on [[Redwood Research]]. That ingest verified rules A and B for its
own batch and passed, because nothing in its verification tested rule C. An ingest can create a page that
discusses a source in prose without owning its evidence, and still pass every check it runs.

### Raw-capture naming drift has returned

**79 of the 365 top-level raw captures** have no `YYYY-MM-DD` filename prefix, and **none of the 79**
carries the raw-source fields the schema requires: `type`, `source_id`, and `captured`. Seventy-four keep
the frontmatter written by the web clipper (title, source, author, published, created). Five have no
frontmatter at all, and four of their summaries are among the 10 that have no source URL.

Git first records 77 of the 79 in October 2026, three of them by renaming an earlier clipping such as
`Untitled.md`, and the other two in September. One of the September pair is the duplicate Cohere capture
that the 2026-09-18 ingest linked to its existing summary. Seventy-eight are cited from the wiki; the
uncited one is the off-domain X post the October 27 ingest deferred at the user's direction.

This is drift the vault has cleared before. [[2026-08-27 Lint Pass]] recorded 66 undated filenames and
normalized them under explicit user authorization. No standing check has tested raw naming since, and no
October pass reported the drift returning. **Nothing was renamed or edited this pass**: raw
captures are immutable unless the user authorizes a metadata correction.

**Superlatives the vault already contradicted.** [[Context Engineering]] called an 80% per-turn token
reduction the largest context-reduction figure in the vault, while the same page records MemCurrent's
8.4K versus 103K input tokens per question, a reduction of about 92%. [[ByteByteGo]] called its Uber Genie
piece the first in the vault to walk through a named internal production system, on a page that already
described DoorDash and Netflix accounts of the same kind. Each claim was removed or rewritten as a
comparison; no figure changed.

**Results that outran their scope.** [[Alpha Signal - The Hidden Memory Tax in AI Agents]] said that
letting a strong model search the raw record was both more accurate and cheaper than reading everything.
The capture shows it was at least as accurate, but cheaper only through one harness, Pi; the Codex and
Claude Code harnesses cost more than full context. The same summary called the study not comparable with
MemCurrent, although MemCurrent also compared memory with full context on three open benchmarks, LoCoMo
among them; it now says the two are comparable only in part. [[Model Routing]] said Cloudflare checked its
randomized model and effort splits against flagged-line changes and blinded model judges. In the capture,
the splits were compared on review outcomes and critical or warning findings, the flagged-line check was
added for effort only, and the judges compared findings from before and after the changes, not the
split arms.
[[Reward Design for RL]], [[Staged Reinforcement Learning Curriculum]], and
[[Group Relative Policy Optimization]] treated verifiable rewards as impossible to corrupt or game, or
passing tests as proof of good behavior, although the reward-design page records a Countdown-Code
environment in which the policy could alter the test it was graded against. All three now say that
passing tests shows good behavior only while the policy cannot alter the tests, and the first two now
describe such rewards as hard, not impossible, to corrupt or game.

**Hardware sources that disagree.** The October 27 Cerebras post and the earlier hardware material differ
in ways the pages had smoothed over. Kimi K3 now carries three layer counts side by side: 92 MoE layers
from Inferact, 93 by attention type (69 linear plus 24 dense) from the Cerebras post, and 23 four-layer
macrocycles from [[@waterloo_intern - From GPT-2 to Kimi K3]]. [[AI Accelerator Architecture]] states that
the vault cannot settle the count. [[Cerebras]] and [[AI Accelerator Architecture]] now separate the post's
4-bit illustration from [[Jacob Peake - AI Chip Architectures]], which says Cerebras computes in 16-bit.
In [[Inference Serving Engines]], the claim that batching matters far less on SRAM-only accelerators now
sits beside Cerebras filling its wafer pipeline with micro-batches. [[KV Cache]] notes that K3's
full-attention layers use Multi-head Latent Attention, so the hybrid-layer saving and latent compression
combine, and that the post does not say whether its cache arithmetic uses the compressed latent.

**One finding was partly rejected.** The hardware pass proposed restricting the
[[AI Accelerator Architecture]] bullet on pipeline bubbles to training. The flashcards do describe bubbles
in training, but their third card shows that keeping every stage busy needs one micro-batch in flight per
stage, which applies to serving as well. The bullet was kept as written. The accepted half reframed the
Cerebras paragraph: serving updates no weights between batches, which is what lets the post fill each
stage with a different request.

**Open questions partly answered.** Thirteen open questions had been overtaken by later ingests. They were
annotated rather than deleted, so each now records the partial answer and what remains open. For example,
[[Chain-of-Thought Monitoring]] said no published work in the vault addresses monitor-aware reasoning,
while citing the closest such evidence in its own gate section. It now says no captured source tests the
question directly, and points to that evidence.

**Stale statements.** Thirteen sentences were true when written and false after later ingests. [[NVIDIA]]
listed three roles and omitted the agent runtime controls recorded from the OpenShell and Sentry posts.
[[Semantic Caching]] said the vault had no measurements, in the same bullet that cites
Cloudflare's production prompt-cache figures; it now says no semantic-cache measurements. [[KV Cache]]'s
prompt-ordering advice now notes that on caches with explicit breakpoints, order alone does not store a
prefix.

### Errors caught in the fixes themselves

Checking every drafted fix against raw caught two precision errors before they were applied:

- Cloudflare's **43%** is the share of **all AI review spend** that went to cache writes before the cost
  work began. A drafted [[KV Cache]] sentence attached it to a single reviewer, and [[Semantic Caching]]
  had stated it ambiguously since the October 27 ingest, directly after that reviewer's figures. Both now
  give the full scope.
- The MemoryTax counterpoint drafted for [[Context Engineering]] and [[Direct Corpus Interaction]] omitted
  the capture's own caveat: only the coding-agent harnesses could read the conversation's photo captions,
  and on the 88 questions whose evidence did not involve photos, Pi scored 100% and full context 99%. Both
  pages now carry the caveat, and *match or beat* became *match*.

The lesson is that a fix makes new claims, and needs the same raw check as an ingest.

The semantic review found one older defect of the same kind: [[Context Engineering]] put its own paraphrase,
that dumping history into the window is expensive rather than thorough, inside quotation marks as though
quoting a source. The marks were removed. The 14 double-quoted passages the fixes added were all found
verbatim in raw captures.

### A publication date the capture contradicts

[[OpenAI - The Hugging Face Incident and the Road Ahead]] gave the post's publication date as 2026-05-12,
taken from the clipper metadata. The post itself says METR and Redwood published their report "today", and
METR's report URL is dated 2026-08-26. The summary now records the date as uncertain and gives both
signals. The capture's metadata was not changed.

## Fixes made

- **Databricks links restored** in 29 files, all as exact inverses of `8deb40a`.
- **Reciprocity cleared** from A 0, B 15, C 16 to zero on all three rules, with no new violations. Across
  the reciprocity and semantic fixes, **24 source IDs** were added to pages that already cited the source
  in prose, **25 links** were added to summaries' `## Affected pages` lists, and **14 links** to
  `## Related pages`. All 24 IDs were already registered, so control parity stays at **361**.
- **57 semantic findings fixed**, one partly fixed, and one low-priority finding deferred. Together, the
  reciprocity and semantic fixes edited 68 pages: 33 concepts, 13 entities, 21 source summaries, and the
  overview. Every edited page carries `updated: 2026-10-27`.
- **Publication-date conflict recorded** in [[OpenAI - The Hugging Face Incident and the Road Ahead]],
  as described above.
- **Lint-report index order corrected.** The newest report had been placed at the top of the index list,
  a block of four August and September reports was reversed, and a blank line split the list. All 43
  existing entries are kept verbatim, now in chronological order, with this report appended.
- Single-source concepts fell from **11 to 10**: [[Direct Corpus Interaction]] gained the MemoryTax source.

Ninety-three existing wiki files changed: 90 content pages plus the three control pages. This report is
the only new page, and no page was deleted. Raw captures, templates, posts, queries, and earlier lint
reports are unchanged.

  `YYYY-MM-DD Author - Title.md`, add `type`, `source_id`, and `captured`, and update every citing link.
- **Add raw naming and raw frontmatter to the standing audit**, so the drift cannot return unnoticed a
  third time.
- **Run the full reciprocity check, including rule C, in every ingest's verification.** The October 27
  ingest passed its own checks while creating five violations.
- **The Kimi K3 layer count is unresolved.** A primary model card or technical report would settle it,
  and with it the Cerebras post's 59-wafer arithmetic, which rests on its own 24 dense layers.
- **The Screwtape and GRAFT papers are not captured.** The vault holds the
  [[Redwood Research - Screwtape - Adversarial Transcript Editing for Monitor Red-Teaming]] post and the
  [[Aria Wong et al - Reducing Reward Hacking by Localizing Bad Behavior Instead of Penalizing It]] post,
  but not the papers behind them.
- **NVIDIA Sentry has not shipped.** Its vendor post gives planned availability early next year; the
  runtime-control claims in [[Agent Observability]] and [[Agent Security and Governance]] should be
  revisited when it does.
- **Deferred low-priority links.** [[Agentic Testing]] could record Cloudflare's acted-on check: a finding
  counted as acted on when the author's next push changed the lines it pointed at, against lines 30 to 40
  away changing about 15% of the time. Such a check shows that the author changed something, not that the
  finding was right. [[Model Routing]] could link the [[Latent-Space Reasoning]] figure that GPT-5.6 Luna uses about
  80% more tokens than Sol at similar performance.
- Standing buckets are non-blocking: 24 legacy lint reports without empty `source_ids`, 34 legacy raw
  links under Citations, 10 summaries with known-missing source URLs, 1 uncited raw capture (the deferred X
  post), 1 reused chronological log heading, 8 short entity pages, and 10 single-source concepts.
