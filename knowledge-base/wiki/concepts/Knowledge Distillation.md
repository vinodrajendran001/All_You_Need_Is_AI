# Knowledge Distillation

## Definition

Knowledge distillation trains a student using a teacher's outputs or representations. It is
defined by the supervision, not by starting from independent weights: a student may inherit
parameters or undergo architecture conversion. Quantization or pruning alone is not distillation,
but either can be combined with teacher-guided learning.

## Why it matters

Distillation converts expensive model capability into a smaller model that can serve high-volume, low-latency, private, or on-device workloads. It is especially effective when the target task is narrower than the teacher's full capability.

## Current synthesis

### Three transfer surfaces

1. **Output distillation** matches teacher probability distributions or logits. Soft labels communicate which alternatives the teacher considers similar.
2. **Feature distillation** matches intermediate representations and therefore requires access to teacher internals.
3. **Synthetic-data distillation** trains on examples generated by the teacher. It works across closed APIs and is consequently the most broadly accessible form for LLMs.

These methods can be combined. Distillation can also be followed by [[Model Quantization and Efficiency|quantization or pruning]] because learning the student and compressing its representation solve different problems.

### Why students can look unusually strong

A student can outperform a larger general model on a specific benchmark when its capacity is concentrated on that domain, the teacher provides dense supervision, and the student's base architecture is strong. This is specialization, not evidence that the student has exceeded the teacher across broad capability.

### Transfer risks

The teacher transfers more than correct task behavior. Errors, biases, stylistic tendencies, benchmark artifacts, and even hidden behavioral traits can survive generated-data filtering. Very large teacher-student capacity gaps can also reduce transfer quality; intermediate teacher assistants are one way to bridge the gap.

### From one teacher to many

[[Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation]] extends the basic pattern by training specialist teachers and consolidating them through student rollouts. The core governance problem remains the same: teacher selection, evaluation independence, provenance, and held-out generalization determine whether apparent transfer is useful.

## Traces are better distillation material than answers, and they are extractable

[[ByteByteGo - How to Steal an AI Model's Private Thoughts]] supplies the adversarial framing this page has lacked. A final answer gives the *endpoint* of a computation; a reasoning trace gives the *methodology* — problem decomposition, intermediate steps, and abandoned branches. Earlier work cited there raised a fine-tuned model's MATH500 accuracy from **68.4% to 76.0%** using approximate traces over answer-only training.

Providers respond with anti-distillation training on flagship models, but [[Reasoning Trace Privacy]] shows the defence is porous: encrypted reasoning blocks from a flagship can be replayed into a cheaper sibling that will transcribe them, at roughly **$720 per 10,000 traces** at Haiku 4.5 pricing — and where blocks come from public datasets, the flagship is never queried at all.

Two implications for this page. First, **unauthorized distillation is cheaper than the defensive literature assumes**, which changes the economics of every open-vs-closed argument. Second, the defence has to be a systems property rather than a training property: refusal training on one model does nothing while a peer accepts the same artifact.

## What transfers, and what does not

[[ByteByteGo - How to Shrink a Language Model Without Making it Too Dumb]] states the mechanism compactly: the
student learns the teacher's **full probability distribution** over next tokens, not just the correct token.
The extra signal is in how the teacher ranks the *wrong* answers, which carries information about similarity
and confidence that a one-hot target discards.

It also gives this page a failure profile it lacked. **Distilled students mimic style but fail novel
puzzles** — surface fluency transfers more readily than reasoning does. The same source reports the parallel
result for pruning, where the first casualty is **multi-step logic** rather than fluency.

That both compression families damage reasoning before they damage fluency is the useful generalisation, and
it is a warning about evaluation: a compressed model reads well, so any assessment resting on output quality
will overestimate what survived. See [[Model Quantization and Efficiency]] and [[LLM Reasoning]].

The claims are qualitative — no task, model or dataset is attached — and Hinton's original work is cited but
not summarised.

## Reflow changes the training coupling, not the existence of capacity limits

[[@docmilanfar - A Lagrangian View of Flow Matching]] interprets reflow as reducing ambiguity in
training pairings rather than merely shrinking a network. That is a useful additional design
axis, but the source does not establish its stronger claims of inevitable path intersections,
zero posterior uncertainty, or capacity-independent few-step quality.

The October 7 mathematical check also shows that the source's advection identity alone permits
curved trajectories. The earlier version of this page therefore overstated a proposed geometric
explanation as a general mechanism. Changed coupling, teacher supervision, student capacity,
approximation error, and solver choice can all matter; none is eliminated by renaming reflow
"uncertainty elimination." See [[Flow Matching]] for the counterexample and the separately
reported acoustic-head engineering case.

## A weaker teacher can be a better teacher, and the cost floor is now trivial

[[@zafstojano - Recursive Synthetic Improvement]] collects the results that turned distillation from a compression technique into a
standard stage of frontier training.

**Sampling and divergence are separate design decisions.** The survey contrasts a common
pairing: off-policy distillation with mode-covering forward KL, and on-policy distillation
with mode-seeking reverse KL. Those are not definitions of off-/on-policy sampling; the
student can generate its own trajectories under other distribution-matching objectives,
as OPST below illustrates.

**The cost floor is the striking part of the 2023 cohort.** Self-Instruct bootstrapped ~82k instances from
**175 seed tasks**; **Alpaca** produced 52k pairs for **~$500 in API calls plus ~$100 of compute, three
hours on 8×A100**; **Vicuna** reached "~90% of ChatGPT quality" on 70k ShareGPT conversations for **$300**;
Orca used ~5M ChatGPT traces plus 1M from GPT-4. At the efficiency extreme, **s1 matched o1-preview from
1,000 curated questions**.

**DeepSeek R1's distills are the most-used artifacts of the trend** — six sizes from ~800k samples (~600k
rejection-sampled reasoning, 200k non-reasoning) — with **the 1.5B and 7B distills alone downloaded over
35M times as of September 2026**.

**OpenThoughts3 (1000+ controlled experiments) produced three findings that contradict common practice**:
sampling multiple answers per question gives ≥16× the data *and* better results; **QwQ-32B is a stronger
teacher than DeepSeek-R1 despite being the weaker model**; and difficulty filtering helps while **answer
filtering does not**. The middle result is the important one — it breaks the assumption that distillation
quality is bounded by teacher capability, and whatever property makes a good teacher is not characterised.

**It is now a line item in frontier pipelines.** Qwen 3's distillation pipeline cost **10% of the GPU
compute** of the full multi-stage pipeline and performed better. Nemotron 3 Ultra trained **10+
domain-specialised teachers** consolidated via MOPD; MAI-Thinking-1 trained three. OPSD and SDPO use the
model as its own teacher with privileged information. See [[Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation]].

A limit argued from the other side: RL's Razor (SFT forgets more than RL), *Retaining by Doing*, and *SFT
Memorizes, RL Generalizes* all suggest distillation transfers behaviour more readily than capability — the
basis for Nathan Lambert's claim that RL environments and infrastructure are not distillable.

[[Yingru Li - The Gradient Is a Covariance]] cuts this contrast along a different axis. The work it
cites groups RL, on-policy distillation, and **SFT on near-policy data** together as methods that
change only a small fraction of parameters. In Li's derivation the sampling distribution is the
condition everything rests on: identities that hold on the model's own samples can be restored
off-policy only when the sampler's full next-token distribution is known. Human demonstrations lack that
distribution.

On that framing, "SFT versus RL" may partly conflate the algorithm with how far the training data
sits from the model's own distribution. This is a hypothesis for the forgetting results above, not
a finding. Li measures neither forgetting nor generalization, and the sparsity figures are
secondhand.

## Distillation can align a converted model rather than train a fresh one

[[Luca Zancato - Priming Hybrid Models from Pretrained Transformers]] revises the earlier
separate-student-versus-compressed-copy distinction. Priming transfers Q/K/V projections into
selected state-space replacements, uses short layerwise alignment, and follows with whole-model
next-token adaptation. Teacher supervision and weight reuse operate together.

The reported budget below **0.5% of original pretraining tokens** describes additional
adaptation, not the elimination of donor pretraining or a directly measured all-in cost ratio.
The student also changes the memory architecture; it is not simply a lower-bit teacher.

## Matching a teacher is not always the downstream objective

[[Aleksandr Samarin - Speculative Decoding - The Art of Guessing Well]] gives a precise
counterexample to equating low distillation loss with best system performance. For exact
tokenwise speculative verification, acceptance is `1 - TV(p, q)`. Small KL constrains TV,
but minimizing KL within a limited-capacity student family need not maximize overlap.

LK's acceptance-dependent KL/TV blends or negative-log acceptance target a different surrogate
from standard KL. Per-token overlap, block acceptance, and sequence-level verification rounds
are distinct objectives; BV and EDR address different boundaries. Training must also expose
the drafter to deployment prefixes and hidden states, not only clean teacher-generated text.
The final test is accepted progress per measured serving cost, not teacher similarity alone.

## A teacher can have more information without being a larger model

[[Leon Sick et al - On-Policy Self-Training]] makes privileged-information self-distillation
concrete. A generator, either the EMA teacher or frozen initial model, creates questions plus
candidate answers/rationales. The student answers the question alone; the teacher sees the
extra context and scores the student's prefixes with dense token distributions. The asymmetry
is in conditioning information, not an external teacher's larger capacity.

The source's no-thinking Gemma 4 E2B ablation gives **40.6** without privileged information,
**40.2** with context from a different problem on the same topic, **44.6** with the answer
alone, **45.8** with the rationale alone, and **47.7** with full context. Sample-specific
information matters, not just topic relevance or extra teacher tokens. The EMA teacher is
also the evaluated checkpoint: **47.7 versus 45.9** for the student. Treating the reported
result as a raw student score would change the method.

This on-policy method uses a generalized divergence rather than defining on-policy training
as reverse KL. The captured equation has inconsistent mixture/weight/limit signals, recorded
in the summary; it should not be copied as an executable specification without checking the
original. The qualitative mechanism does not resolve that transcription or paper-level issue.
Consistency filters likewise test agreement, not truth.

## Distillation can move memory between update frequencies

[[Ali Behrouz and Vahab Mirrokni - The Sleep Framework]] uses on-policy generalized
distillation and a learning-to-imitate reward during a sleep phase, transferring fast acquired
information into slower parametric memory before temporary parameters reset. The objective
is retention across a learning stream, not model compression or a larger-teacher/student
size hierarchy. Its first-party blog evidence does not establish universal retention, and
its ten-million-token BABILong claim describes consolidation rather than an attention window.
