# 2026-09-11 Lint Pass

Thirty-third comprehensive lint pass, run the same day as the eleven-source September 11 ingest.

## Scope

436 wiki pages, 240 raw captures, 215 source summaries, 110 concept pages, 68 entity pages, 32 prior lint
reports. Checks run: frontmatter parse, wikilink resolution, control-page source-ID parity, affected-page
reciprocity, section order, `## Related pages` presence, orphans, summary-bypass, tag vocabulary, raw-capture
traceability, numeric consistency, and a qualifier-propagation audit.

## Findings

### 1. Three separate defects, one shared cause: the check verified a proxy, not the invariant

This is the finding worth keeping. Three unrelated-looking defects below all survived multiple prior lint
passes for the same reason — in each case a check existed, passed, and was measuring something adjacent to
the property that actually mattered.

| Invariant | What was checked instead | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Tag vocabulary is unified | Tag *spelling* and case (`AI` vs `ai`) | Namespace fork went unseen for 12 days |
| Three control pages agree on source IDs | Every raw `source_id` appears *somewhere* in the wiki | `log.md` silently 9 IDs short for 12 days |
| `raw/sources/` holds N captures | Recursive file walk of `raw/sources/` | Counts inflated by 40 non-captures |

The pattern: a check passes, so the area is presumed healthy, so the next pass reuses the check. A check that
has never failed is not evidence of health — it may simply be pointed slightly off-target. Each of the three
below was found only by asking the invariant directly.

### 2. The tag vocabulary had re-forked along a namespace axis

50 pages carried `topic/*` tags, and **43 of 48 distinct `topic/*` tags had an exact flat twin** — `topic/agents`
(15) beside `agents` (11), `topic/inference` (11) beside `inference` (36), and so on.

Three details make this worse than a cosmetic drift:

- **The 2026-08-25 pass had already unified the tag vocabulary.** This is a decision that was made, applied, and
  then silently reversed.
- **It was reversed by ingests, not by an edit.** The fork begins 2026-08-30 and runs *through the middle of
  individual batches*: on 2026-08-30, 9 pages in one batch got `topic/*` and 10 got flat tags. The September 11
  batch was 100% `topic/*` (28 of 28) — self-inflicted, the same day as this pass.
- **Three lint passes (08-31, 09-03, 09-04) ran during the fork and did not see it**, because each checked
  whether tags disagreed on *spelling*, never whether they disagreed on *namespace*.

### 3. `log.md` was 9 source IDs short, and the body was correct the whole time

Control-page parity failed: `index.md` 233, `wiki/overviews/AI Knowledge Base Overview.md` 233, `log.md` **224**.

All 9 missing IDs came from a single batch — the 2026-08-30 nine-source ingest — and they sat contiguously at
positions 200–208 of `index.md`. `log.md`'s **body** carries a complete, correct entry for that ingest naming
all 9 sources and all 9 IDs. Only its **frontmatter** was skipped.

Two things this exposes:

- **A half-applied ingest step 5 is invisible to a reader.** The narrative record was perfect; only the machine-
  readable index was wrong. Nothing a human would notice while reading the log.
- **The 2026-08-31 lint pass ran the very next day and passed.** So did 09-03 and 09-04, and so did the September
  11 ingest's own closing validation, which reported "exact parity" — that verification was itself wrong. The
  reachability check (`every raw source_id appears somewhere in the wiki`) is true and stays true even when the
  three control pages disagree, so it cannot detect this class of defect.

### 4. A summary discarded its source's entire substance while retaining the caveat about it

`Alpha Signal - Why Tabular Foundation Models Are a Huge Unlock` was 143 words against a 1,097-word capture. It
named three models with no vendor, no architecture and no figures — and then closed with the note that its
"scale and speed claims are source-reported and need primary-paper verification."

**The qualifier survived; the claims it qualified did not.** What the capture contains and the summary dropped:

- **TabFM (Google Research)** — omitted entirely, despite being the first model discussed: alternating row and
  column attention, trained wholly on synthetic data, Apache 2.0, and being integrated natively into BigQuery.
- **KumoRFM is NVIDIA's** — the summary gave no vendor and did not record that NVIDIA acquired Kumo in June 2026
  and folded it into its Structured Data and Graph Models ecosystem.
- **TabICL's only hard numbers** — up to 500,000 samples and 500 features, up to 10× faster than TabPFNv2.
- **The mechanism** — why serialising a table into an LLM fails: permutation invariance, tokenizers splitting
  `100.50` into three tokens and destroying column-wise distributions, and context/compute blowup.
- **The dual-stack roadmap** — TFM for prototyping and cold-start, XGBoost once the schema stabilises, with
  real-time card-fraud APIs and high-frequency trading named as cases where a TFM does not fit.

This is the **exact inverse** of the 2026-09-04 finding. That pass found facts propagating across pages while
their qualifiers were left behind. Here a single page kept the qualifier and dropped the fact — which is the
more corrosive direction, because the page still *reads* as careful.

### 5. The qualifier rule held under its first real test

The September 11 batch created a genuine hazard: two sources reporting **"1.41×"** meaning different things —
SemiAnalysis's GDN v3 **kernel-level decode** speedup, and Cohere's **end-to-end AIME 2025** result — landing in
adjacent page neighbourhoods (`Megakernels`, `Inference Serving Engines`, `Linear Attention and Recurrent Memory`).

Audited all five carrying pages. **Every instance names its model and its scope**, and the kernel-level figure is
explicitly marked "kernel-level figures only, with no end-to-end number given." **No fix required.** A 21-pair
number/qualifier audit across the batch also came back clean.

This is the first positive evidence that the rule introduced after the 2026-09-04 diagnosis works, and it held at
precisely the point where it was load-bearing.

### 6. Counting `raw/sources/` recursively inflates every capture statistic by 40

`raw/sources/` contains 240 dated captures **plus two nested corpora** — `pocketflow-tutorial-docs/` (39 files)
and `pytorch-practice/` (1 file). Both are legitimate, wiki-referenced vault content, but neither follows the
capture naming convention and none of the 40 files carries a `source_id`.

Any check that walks the directory recursively reports **280 captures and 86 missing `source_id`** instead of the
true **240 and 46**. This pass produced both figures before catching it. The hazard is operational, not cosmetic:
a future "ingest the new files in `raw/`" run that walks recursively will surface 40 files that look un-ingested
and are not.

### 7. A lint report failed its own audit

`2026-06-04 Lint Pass` contains the table row `Missing ## Related pages | 0` — and was itself the only page in
the vault missing `## Related pages`.

### 8. Raw captures without `source_id`: 46 of 240 (19%)

All from 2026-05 to 2026-08, concentrated in June (20) and July (22). Raw captures are immutable per the schema,
so this is **reported, not fixed**.

Framing matters: this is a schema-conformance gap, not a traceability defect. **All 240 captures are
link-reachable from a summary (0 unreachable), and all 194 `source_id`s that do exist appear in the wiki.** The
invariant that matters holds at 100%.

### 9. The privacy check broke the invariant it was certifying

The [[2026-09-04 Lint Pass]] recorded: *"4 query pages on disk, 3 tracked, 1 local-only
(`<title>`). It is referenced from no tracked page. **PASS.**"*

Lint reports are tracked and committed. By naming the page in order to document that nothing named it, that pass
**created the reference it was certifying absent** — and then committed it. The assertion was false at the moment
it was written, and false *because* it was written.

This is the fourth instance of finding 1, and the sharpest: the other three checks pointed slightly off-target,
while this one was self-refuting. A check that writes its subject into a tracked artifact cannot verify that the
subject is absent from tracked artifacts.

**Fixed:** the title is redacted from the 2026-09-04 report, with a note recording why. The leak was plain text in
backticks rather than a wikilink, so it created no graph edge — but the schema forbids local-only query page
*titles* on tracked pages, not merely links to them. This report deliberately refers to the page only as "the
local-only query page".

## Fixes made

- **Unified the tag vocabulary (81 pages, two passes).** Pass 1: 52 pages, `topic/X` → `X`, `llm/wiki` → `wiki`,
  `AI` → `ai`, deduped, preserving each page's inline-vs-block YAML style. Pass 2: 29 pages, merging synonym
  duplicates `agents` → `ai-agents`, `research-automation` → `automated-research`, and `tools`/`tool` → `tool-use`.
  The second pass was necessary because the first had *grown* the pre-existing `agents` vs `ai-agents` fork from
  11 pages to 26. **Result: 348 → 301 distinct tags, 0 namespace forks, 0 case forks, 0 parse failures.**
- **Retained `source/summary` (215 uses) as the sole namespaced tag, deliberately.** It is a *type* tag, it has no
  flat twin, and it partitions the vault by page kind rather than subject. Recording the decision here so it is not
  re-merged by a future pass reading "0 namespaced tags" as the target.
- **Backfilled the 9 missing source IDs into `log.md` frontmatter**, inserted at their `index.md` positions. All
  three control pages now agree at **233**.
- **Rebuilt `Alpha Signal - Why Tabular Foundation Models Are a Huge Unlock`**, 143 → 1,053 words, restoring the
  four named models with vendors and architectures, the failure analysis, the dual-stack roadmap, and the figures
  the caveat was pointing at — with provenance kept on every number.
- **Rebuilt `Tabular Foundation Models`**, 176 → 829 words, with the canonical spine (`Definition` / `Why it
  matters` / `Current synthesis` / `Open questions` / `Related pages`) it was missing three sections of. Added the
  explicit note that accuracy is never the source's argument, so no claim that TFMs beat gradient-boosted trees on
  predictive quality is supported by this vault.
- **Added `## Related pages` to `2026-06-04 Lint Pass`.**
- **Redacted the local-only query page's title from the `2026-09-04 Lint Pass` report** and left a note there
  explaining that naming it was what created the tracked reference the report certified absent.
- **Made the wikilink checker code-fence aware.** The previous checker reported 14 broken links and 1 wrapped
  link; all 15 were Python in fenced blocks (`[[0] * (n + 1) ...]`, `[[nums[i] for i in ...]]`). True count is **0
  and 0**.

## Follow-ups

- **Fix the checks, not just the data.** Three defects here were invisible to checks that passed. The three
  corrected checks — namespace-aware tag comparison, direct three-way control-page parity, and top-level-only
  capture enumeration — should be the ones carried into the next pass, and the closing validation of every *ingest*
  should run the parity check in its three-way form rather than the reachability form.
- **The September 11 batch caused the tag fork it was audited for.** Ingests are writing `topic/*`; the convention
  lives only in prior pages and in lint reports. Until the ingest step itself reads the existing vocabulary, this
  will re-fork.
- **Re-audit summary-to-capture compression.** Finding 4 was found by hand, not by a check. 32 summaries remain
  under 300 words; each is a candidate for the same defect, and the pattern to look for is a surviving caveat with
  no surviving claim.
- **Unchanged backlog:** 2 concept pages under 250 words (`Autonomous Driving Systems` 200w, `Defensive Deception
  for Open Models` 205w); 21 single-source concept pages; **24 of 110 concept pages missing a canonical section**,
  still worst among the largest (`AI Agents in Production` 4,208w missing 3, `Agent Security and Governance`
  3,823w missing 2); 102 pages using inline `tags: [...]` against the schema's block-list form; summary
  raw-capture pointer style still split (heading 181, bullet 178) though **every summary does link its capture**.
- **The 46 captures without `source_id`** need an explicit user decision, since raw captures are immutable absent
  one.
