## Findings

### Illustrative timings had become measurements

[[Mayank Pratap Singh - Speculative Decoding in vLLM]] and [[Speculative Decoding]] called
the **2.5x versus approximately 1.8x** system-tax example measured, even though the capture's
Figure 26 labels it illustrative. The **52% to 74% verification share** and **24% to 10%
draft share** in Figure 28 are also explicitly illustrative. The batch-128 straggler range
belongs to that pedagogical discussion, not the article's actual experiment.

Corrected the claims where they appear, not only in a late caveat. Kept the actual negative
result separate: **Llama-3.1-8B on a single 48 GB Ada GPU**, slower with EAGLE3 in greedy
batch 1 and a non-greedy, approximately 500-concurrent load test, with reported acceptance
length **1.81**. Only the latter regime is described as saturated. The author's proposed
tuning remains a proposal, not a successful rerun or an ablation of the causes.

### Decoder correctness and serving heuristics had become guarantees

Reconciled the older Mayank and [[ByteByteGo - How to Make LLMs 3X Faster]] summaries with
the newer synthesis:

- Greedy matching uses a target correction; stochastic exact sampling requires the appropriate
  acceptance and residual rules. Equal output distributions do not promise seed-identical
  wording or factually correct answers.
- The geometric tau formula includes the correction/bonus token. Its examples use **K=5**;
  one-token progress does not mean a round cannot be slower than ordinary decoding.
- Low-batch dense weight traffic is a motivating case, not the definition of all decode.
  Cache-bound large batches can behave differently, and batch size alone is not an on/off rule.
- Prompt lookup avoids separate draft-model weights, not every allocation. Released compatible
  heads do not require the serving operator to train them from scratch.
- No direct prefill acceleration is not invariant time to first token. Restored the raw's
  **roughly** qualifier and the effects of initialization, queueing, and resource competition.
- Workload labels, temperature, and roughly 50% acceptance are not universal break-even rules.
  Concurrency-only controls are historical examples, not a complete account of current tuning.

[[Arithmetic Intensity and the Roofline Model]] and the speculation concept now keep
attention-core arithmetic estimates separate from measured end-to-end latency and actual
kernel dispatch. A locally served model does not automatically provide free speculative
sub-agent compute.

Preserved the unresolved DeepSeek measurement boundary: the ByteByteGo article reports both
roughly **1.8x generation throughput** and a separate statement about slightly lower throughput
with better end-to-end latency. It does not align those operating points. The second-token
**80-90%** acceptance figure is not a constant acceptance probability for every draft slot.

### Closing a trace had become disabling reasoning

[[Sebastian Raschka - Controlling Reasoning Effort in LLMs]] previously said Qwen3's empty
thinking block meant the model could not reason. The capture describes selecting a no-trace
mode, not disabling all hidden computation.

Corrected the summary and aligned [[Reasoning Effort Control]], [[LLM Reasoning]],
[[Reasoning Compression]], [[Test-Time Scaling]], the Raschka entity, and the catalogs.
Learned modes, external stopping budgets, and internal iteration controls are distinct.
Delimiters do not create reasoning ability, but that does not make every generated reasoning
token merely cosmetic.

### A trained policy comparison had become a causal token ablation

The Raschka capture discusses Toggle in its Kimi K2.5 section, but explicitly attributes the
reported evaluation to **K2 Thinking**: **25-30% fewer generated tokens with little change in
benchmark performance**. Its figure/section framing is broader than that explicit tested-model
attribution, which the repaired pages now retain.

Removed the inference that this proves the original tokens were unnecessary and the stronger
claim of no accuracy cost. Training can reorganize computation; comparing trained policies
does not identify deletable segments of a fixed policy's trace.

Also narrowed the selector claim: Toggle has no runtime selector between its two training
phases, but K2.5 has a separate **thinking/instant** switch. The summary, reasoning concepts,
[[Chain-of-Thought Monitoring]], and overview now preserve both distinctions.

### Monitoring evidence had acquired stronger causal conclusions

[[Chain-of-Thought Monitoring]] now distinguishes a possible change in telemetry from a
measured change in detection. Toggle did not measure monitorability. A greater-capacity,
shorter-trace hypothesis is not proof that every capability gain erodes monitoring.

Retained the reported Astra monitoring regression while removing claims that its precise
cause is established or every architectural contribution has been ruled out. Retained
OpenAI's retrospective early-detection account as first-party evidence of potential
sensitivity, not deployed coverage during the incident.

[[Anthropic - Would This Change Your Answer (CHIVE)]] reports no uplift from the tested
activation-reading tools over a transcript baseline. That is not a universal proof that
transcripts are the more informative signal or a sufficient monitor. Added the existing
Raschka-effort and CHIVE IDs to the monitoring concept, with reciprocal affected-page links
in their summaries; no new evidence source was created.

### Metadata and catalog descriptions lagged the bodies

Changed [[Qwen]] from `entity_kind: organization` to `entity_kind: model-family`, matching
the page's definition rather than classifying the family as Alibaba itself.

The index and overview no longer promise opposite prefill/decode bottlenecks, disaggregation
benefits regardless of transfer cost, lossless reasoning compression, or exclusively low-load
speculation. The Raschka catalog description now distinguishes the different control recipes
rather than saying all six models install the same low/medium/high interface.
