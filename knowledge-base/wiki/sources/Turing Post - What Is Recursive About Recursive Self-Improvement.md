# Turing Post - What Is Recursive About Recursive Self-Improvement

## Summary

This essay distinguishes repeated AI-assisted optimization from stronger recursive self-improvement.
An ordinary loop proposes a change, runs it, evaluates it with fixed external criteria, and retains or
rejects it. Recursion becomes stronger when the editable object expands to search strategy, experiment
design, compute allocation, environment adaptation, and eventually the evaluator itself.

## Key claims

- AI4AI-Bench is cited as a bounded example in which agents receive several hours to modify real
  training algorithms and the result is rerun from scratch.
- Five autonomy surfaces are distinguished: execution, strategy selection, experience acquisition,
  environment adaptation, and improvement of the improvement process.
- Faster or more automated development is not sufficient for RSI while objectives, permissions,
  compute limits, or evaluation remain fixed.
- Allowing a system to revise the evaluator that certifies progress creates a regress; some tests,
  judgments, or formal checks must remain independently auditable.

## Why it matters

The decisive question is not whether AI is involved in improving AI, but what remains outside the
editable region. This reframes RSI as a governance and measurement boundary rather than a claim about
secret motives or self-preservation.

## Tensions and caveats

The source reports no recursive depth, acceleration rate, benchmark score, or self-sustaining
capability growth. Its discussion of current systems being easy to stop is qualified, and the cited
research taxonomies need their own primary-source review.
