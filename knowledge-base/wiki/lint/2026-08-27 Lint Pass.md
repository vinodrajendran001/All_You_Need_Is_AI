# 2026-08-27 Lint Pass

Full-vault health check run after the Granite 4.2 / speculative-decoding ingest brought the tracked
registry to 200 source IDs.

State at the start of the pass: 349 wiki pages, 210 raw source captures, 200 tracked source IDs,
**0 structural defects**. State after: 350 wiki pages (this report), same 200 IDs, 0 structural
defects, and 394 previously-invisible false claims corrected.

## Scope

Link integrity, frontmatter and marker-tag conformance, section ordering, control-page consistency,
orphan detection, raw-store naming and tagging, thin and single-source pages, near-duplicate concept
names, the privacy invariant on `wiki/queries/`, and — new in this pass — **reciprocity of the
`## Affected pages` claim on every source summary**.

## Headline finding

**The `## Affected pages` heading had no definition, so its meaning forked — and 394 of its claims
were false.**

`templates/source-summary.md` listed `## Affected pages` as a bare heading with no gloss. With no
stated meaning, the section came to be used two incompatible ways across the vault:

1. as a **record** of pages this ingest actually changed, and
2. as a **routing list** of pages the source is merely relevant to.

Under reading (1) the section is a verifiable audit trail. Under reading (2) it is a topic tag. The
vault had been silently mixing them, which made the section untrustworthy for either purpose: a
reader following an affected link had no way to know whether the target page would actually mention
the source.

Measured across all 182 source summaries, **394 listed pages did not cite the source at all** —
neither its `source_id` nor a link back to the summary. These break down as:

- **303** from the 63-lesson AI Builder Club batch, whose summaries each claimed 4–5 concept pages
  while the batch's real integration point is the synthesis page.
- **91** from 44 individually-ingested sources.

This is the same class of defect as the 2026-08-26 headline finding, but an order of magnitude
larger, and it was invisible to every previous lint pass because no check had ever tested the claim.

### The check itself needed debugging

Worth recording, because the first run was wrong in a way that looked authoritative. Matching only
on `source_id` reported **403** violations. That over-counted: a page can legitimately honour a
source by wikilinking the summary page instead of quoting its ID. Counting both forms of citation
gave the true figure of 394. A reciprocity checker that recognises only one citation style
manufactures false positives at roughly a 2% rate here, which is more than enough to send a fix in
the wrong direction.

## Findings

### Fixed in this pass

1. **394 unhonoured `## Affected pages` claims** across 107 sources — see headline. Now 0.
2. **5 sources with no real integration at all.** Their summaries existed and were indexed, but no
   concept, entity, or synthesis page cited them, so they were unreachable by topic. These were the
   three-part [[Giles Thomas - Why GPT-2 Weights Beat Mine? Part 1]] series,
   [[Netflix - In-House LLM Serving]], and
   [[Zhe Ren et al - Self-Improvements in Modern Agentic Systems]].
3. **5 pages with a content section ordered after `## Open questions`.** Caused by an ingest helper
   that anchors new sections on `## Open questions` when present and `## Related pages` otherwise;
   passing the wrong anchor appends *after* the open questions instead of before them. Affected
   [[KV Cache]], [[LLM Inference]], [[Model Quantization and Efficiency]],
   [[Serving Benchmarks and Goodput]], and [[Real-Time Voice AI]] — four of them introduced by the
   immediately preceding ingest, one older. Reorder verified lossless by word-multiset comparison
   against `HEAD`.
4. **11 source summaries with no `## Affected pages` section at all**, now populated from their
   computed true integration sets.
5. **The template gloss**, so the fork cannot recur.
6. **4 source summaries that existed locally but were never staged into git.** They are referenced
   from `index.md`, so any fresh clone of the repository would have had four broken links. Not
   git-ignored — simply never added. All four have `?` or `@` in their filenames, which is the likely
   reason the routine commit path skipped them. Now staged.

### Not defects, but worth writing down

- **8 raw `source_id`s are absent from the 200-ID registry.** These are the constituent articles of
  [[ByteByteGo - System Design and AI at Scale (May 2026 Batch)]], a composite summary covering eight
  captures. A prior lint deliberately resolved this by declaring the eight IDs *on the summary page*
  while the registry counts the composite as one. That decision stands; this note exists so the next
  pass does not re-flag it as a gap. A registry check should test "claimed by some wiki page", not
  "present in `index.md`".
- The raw-store convention drift originally recorded here — 66 undated filenames and 69 missing
  `source/raw` tags — was **normalized later in this pass under explicit user authorization**. See
  *Raw-store normalization* below.

## Fixes made

- Integrated the 5 orphaned sources into 9 concept pages: the Netflix source into
  [[Inference Serving Engines]], [[ML Systems at Scale]], and [[Serving Benchmarks and Goodput]]; the
  self-improvement survey into [[Recursive Self-Improvement]], [[Agent Skill]], and
  [[Agent Memory]]; the GPT-2 series into [[LLM Training Pipeline]], [[LLM-as-a-Judge]], and
  [[Benchmark Optimization]].
- Rewrote `## Affected pages` on **162** summaries to the computed true set and added the section to
  **11** more. Annotations such as `- [[X]] - new` were preserved wherever the link survived.
- **Relocated 325 merely-relevant links** into each summary's existing `## Related pages`; a further
  130 were already present there and were simply dropped. No link was deleted outright — the
  correction moves information rather than discarding it.
- Reordered the 5 mis-anchored sections.

### Raw-store normalization

Raw captures are immutable by default, so this was done only after the user confirmed it. The
governing question was whether normalizing destroys anything. It does not, and the reason is worth
recording: **every component of the corrected filename already existed as data inside the vault.**

- **66 files renamed** to `YYYY-MM-DD Author - Title.md`. The date and author came from each file's
  own frontmatter (`created` / `captured` / `published`, and `author`); the existing title portion of
  the filename was preserved verbatim. The rename therefore only surfaces metadata the file already
  carried.
- **5 of the 66 had no frontmatter at all** — Alpha Signal captures stored as bare article text. Their
  date and author were recovered from the `source_id` and `source_author` of the wiki summary that
  links to them, and full schema-conformant frontmatter was written.
- **70 inbound wikilinks rewritten** across 62 files, each as `[[New Name|Old Display Text]]` so that
  no rendered page reads differently than before. Verified: 0 broken links afterwards, and none of
  the 66 old names survives anywhere as a link target.
- **64 files gained the `source/raw` marker tag and 49 gained `type: raw-source`.** Existing tags were
  kept, including the `clippings` tag that records Obsidian Web Clipper provenance — the marker was
  added alongside, never substituted.
- **1 pre-existing YAML syntax error fixed**: `title: Reinforcement Learning: An Overview` in the
  Kevin Murphy capture was an unquoted scalar containing a colon, which is invalid YAML and would
  have failed any frontmatter parse. Now quoted. All 560 raw and wiki frontmatter blocks parse.

Two extraction bugs were caught before anything was written, both worth recording because each
produced confident, wrong-looking-correct output:

1. A naive `^author:\s*(.*)$` regex let `\s*` consume the newline after an empty `author:` field and
   capture the *next* frontmatter line, yielding names like `published: 2026-04-22`. Fixed by parsing
   the block properly rather than with a single-line regex.
2. The fallback that recovers author from an owning summary matched on "summary text contains the
   title", which found the wrong summary and attributed a Perplexity Research article to
   `Unknown (LinkedIn post)`. Fixed by requiring an exact `[[wikilink]]` to the capture.
- Added a gloss to `templates/source-summary.md` defining `## Affected pages` as pages the ingest
  actually changed, requiring reciprocal citation, excluding the control pages, and directing
  merely-relevant links to `## Related pages`.

## Follow-ups

- **Promote the reciprocity check into the standard post-ingest recipe.** It is the only check that
  catches a summary claiming credit for work that was never done, and it must count both citation
  styles.
- Carried forward from [[2026-08-26 Lint Pass]]: the AI Builder Club batch remains one synthesis
  against 84 concept pages. This pass makes the asymmetry *honest* — the 63 summaries now point at
  the synthesis they actually fed — but does not change the underlying shape.
