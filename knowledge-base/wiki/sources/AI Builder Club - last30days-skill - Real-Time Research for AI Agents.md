# AI Builder Club - last30days-skill: Real-Time Research for AI Agents

## Summary

This article profiles `last30days-skill`, an Agent Skill that coordinates searches across social, developer, video, news-discussion, and prediction-market sources, then synthesizes a recent-topic brief. The tool emphasizes current community activity and engagement signals rather than relying only on general web search or model training data. Optional credentials and browser sessions expand the available sources.

The source describes pre-search entity resolution, parallel retrieval, cross-source story clustering, comparison workflows, shareable reports, and secondary ranking of notable commentary. Suggested uses include meeting preparation, competitive research, fast-moving technology monitoring, and current travel or purchase research.

## Key claims

- A skill can package a multi-tool research workflow that would otherwise require manually coordinating many platform-specific searches.
- Freshness and community access address a different problem from static model knowledge.
- Parallel retrieval reduces elapsed time, while cluster merging reduces duplicate coverage of the same event.
- Engagement can provide useful evidence of community attention, but it is not equivalent to truth, representativeness, or importance.
- Bring-your-own credentials and browser sessions expand reach while introducing privacy, account, and terms-of-service concerns.
- Portable Agent Skills can distribute not only knowledge but a repeatable research procedure and output format.

## Why it matters

The profile illustrates [[Agent Skill]] as an orchestration artifact layered over [[Tool Use and Function Calling]], [[Search-Augmented Language Models]], and the [[Agentic Loop]]. It also demonstrates a central [[Context Engineering]] pattern: gather broadly outside the final context, deduplicate and rank, then pass a compact synthesis and evidence set to the model.

## Tensions / open questions

- Engagement-ranked sources can amplify popularity, controversy, spam, or coordinated manipulation rather than reliable evidence.
- Coverage across closed platforms depends on fragile APIs, scraping, browser sessions, and changing platform policies.
- A synthesized brief needs explicit provenance and uncertainty to prevent cross-source aggregation from laundering rumors into facts.
- Popularity and star counts are time-sensitive source claims, not quality guarantees.
