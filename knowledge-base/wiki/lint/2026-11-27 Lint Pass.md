# 2026-11-27 Lint Pass

## Scope

Forty-ninth pass, following the November 27 ingest of 26 sources. The starting inventory was
**773 typed wiki pages**. Adding this report leaves **774**, including **443 source summaries**,
**149 concepts**, **110 entities**, and **49 lint reports**, with **469 owned source IDs**.
Local-only query files are excluded.

Structural coverage repeated the standing audit: frontmatter and directory/type agreement,
source ownership, index/log/overview parity, wikilink resolution, reachability, index
categories, Related-pages endings, source-summary sections, source/post/query boundaries, and
affected-page/body-citation reciprocity. A supplementary survey checked canonical metadata across
all **475 top-level raw captures**. The publisher check compared each source summary's author
with the matching entity page in both directions. The ingest's three new pages,
[[AI Consciousness and Moral Patienthood]], [[Antithesis]], and [[intelligentLearn]], were
compared with their nearest existing pages. None has a same-topic twin, and the seven concept and
entity pages that discuss machine consciousness or moral patienthood all link to the new concept.

Semantic review covered all 26 new source summaries, the three new pages, and the consumer edits
the ingest made to existing pages. Seven read-only reviewers compared those pages with the raw
captures, and every finding was then checked against the raw text before any edit. A
propagation scan of the flattened text of the whole wiki looked for each corrected claim
elsewhere. The pass did **not** revalidate every older summary, reproduce benchmarks, execute
captured code, or fetch external papers.

The preservation baseline was the knowledge-base working tree after the finished ingest, not
Git HEAD.

## Findings

### The graph was clean; the claims were not

The structural audit returned zero findings before and after the fixes, apart from the deferred
off-domain capture `Post by @Tristan0x on X`, which stays uncited by design. All **774 pages**
are reachable afterward, index/log/overview parity holds at **469 IDs**, and affected-page
reciprocity is **A 0 / B 0**. Body-citation reciprocity is **C 0**: no concept, entity, or
synthesis cites a source summary in its body without that source's ID.

Semantic review confirmed **66 findings**: **28 errors**, **18 missing qualifiers**, **8
overclaims**, **7 misattributions**, **4 internal contradictions**, and **1 arithmetic slip**.
None was rejected on recheck, and all were fixed. The propagation scan found copies of five
findings beyond the reviewed lines: the Tomassetti summary's model-role sentence (G3-1), the
Huntley summary's "deterministic" tools (G3-6), the Part 4 summary's missing Orca exception
(G4-3), the Arush summary's "identity self-reports" (G7-2), and the DoorDash "no baselines"
wording in the index and overview (G5-11). Each was corrected under its original finding.

### Readings were reversed

- **Prompting style:** [[Frank Rousseau - Agentic Coding on an Open-Source Project]] said he
  lets the model be "a bit evasive". In the raw, the vagueness is his own prompting technique,
  which leaves the model room to be creative (G1-1).
- **Stall order:** [[GPU Execution Model]] said long scoreboard and barrier led before
  `cp.async` and math pipe throttle led after it. Lewis Won's profile shows math pipe throttle
  already leading before `cp.async`; afterwards long scoreboard leaves the top four (G7-1).
- **Restart gap:** [[Test-Time Scaling]] traced Zanichelli's restart advantage to stale context
  "rather than to token accounting". The author credits part of it to token accounting and says
  token counting is not the whole story (G2-1).
- **Privileged pod:** [[Kubernetes]] called Vinod's nested-cluster pod unprivileged. Only its
  namespace was; the pod itself ran as root and privileged (G5-1).
- **Fastest clock:** [[Loop Engineering]] placed the soak-test faults at the run's fastest
  clock. They appeared at 231.25 MHz, after earlier images had run at 250 and 237.5 MHz (G5-6).

### Comparisons were stated beyond their tables

- **Harness adapters:** five pages said "each specialist is ahead on four of six pairs", which
  cannot hold for adapters that each have two pairs. The specialists lead on four pairs, tie on
  OpenCode Terminal-Bench 2.1, and trail on Hermes SWE-bench Pro (**43.1%** against **47.1%**)
  (G2-11). The transfer section's base scores are no longer called separate runs as fact: two of
  the four appear in the main tables (G2-2). [[Harness Optimization]] now records orthogonality
  as "the best case for merging" rather than as what makes concatenation possible (G2-6).
- **Swarm results:** the **54 of 70** count covers teams of 2 to 8, not teams of 8 (G2-7).
  Self-organized teams beat protocol teams for every model on git-to-zig only (G2-8). The early
  compaction range belongs to self-organized teams (G2-10), and context staleness is the author's
  explanation, not an established cause (G2-9).
- **Inference chip:** [[AI Accelerator Architecture]] said most large gains came from data
  movement. Burst KV reads were the largest step (**1.74×**), but most other large steps changed
  compute (G5-8). Per dollar, the 4-thread CPU twin and the T4 CUDA port beat the FPGA while the
  32-thread twin did not (G5-2). The summary called the "195×" row a rounding of a quotient that
  rounds to 194; it now says the printed 1.1 tok/s is presumably rounded (G5-7).
- **Routing reproduction:** random routing's hit rates also rose slightly with pool size
  (multiturn 0.43→0.47), so "only Kinference's hit rates rise" was false (G5-3). The agentic-mix
  extension was 18 runs, not one (G5-4), and the benchmark pools used six GPUs of the shared
  8-GPU node (G5-5).
- **Serving benchmarks:** the candidate-pool comparison belongs to the RTX PRO 6000 protocol and
  names only vLLM-GR and NVIDIA SID-GR (G5-10). The reproduction states its shared-prefix limit
  in words but gives no number for it (G5-13). The series' **172** papers are its in-scope
  corpus, not papers it cites (G4-6).

### Hedges and conditions were dropped

- **"Can't prove":** Ankit Gupta's retrospective presents the 38 lost hours as a possible cost it
  "can't prove"; [[AI-Generated Kernels]] and [[AI Accelerator Architecture]] had stated it as
  established (G5-9).
- **intelligentLearn:** a burst that ends before a replica starts "may" point to L1 (G4-9).
  Model precision stays fixed unless quality and throughput at the new precision are part of the
  result (G4-7). Fusion "can" reduce launch overhead, and FlashAttention keeps "nearly unchanged"
  semantics (G4-8). The mechanism claims rest on the deeply read tier except continuous batching,
  whose only source, Orca, sits outside the corpus (G4-3).
- **DoorDash:** the latency figure is compared with "minutes under heavy load" in the old system,
  so the reported figures lack quantified baselines rather than any baseline (G5-11, six sites).
  The coordinator becomes a bottleneck only if it is overloaded (G5-12).
- **Other dropped conditions:** an interactive card is one example channel for a deferred
  approval (G1-10). A later fsync succeeds although the data never reached the disk and is lost
  at the next power failure unless rewritten (G3-7). Miksovsky's Microsoft engineer spoke
  "occasionally" and such engineers "could" take a long time (G3-8). Removing a workspace "could"
  make a model fail a theory's conditions with more advanced methods (G7-5), and Hoel's paper
  repurposes existing techniques and relays one ablation rather than testing nothing (G7-4).
