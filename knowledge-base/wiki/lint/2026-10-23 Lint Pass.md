# 2026-10-23 Lint Pass

## Scope

Forty-second comprehensive pass. The starting inventory was **598 typed wiki pages**, counted from the
working tree. Adding this report leaves **599**, including **316 source summaries**, **136 concepts**,
**88 entities**, and **42 lint reports**. Evidence ownership is **342 source IDs**. The raw collection
contains **344 top-level captures** and **413 files**.

Structural coverage was the standing audit: frontmatter schema and directory/type agreement, source
ownership and exact index/log/overview parity, link resolution and reachability, index categories and
uncited captures, Related-pages endings, heading structure, tags, and the source/post/private-query
boundaries. It returned **zero findings** at the start of the pass and zero at the end.

The semantic review traced **all 25 source chains** added by the October 23 ingest — raw capture →
source summary → every consumer page — across **118 consumer edges**, using six parallel read-only
tracing passes partitioned by subject area. Each traced claim was checked for strengthening in transit,
invented or drifted numbers, dropped qualifiers, misattribution, and silently resolved contradictions.

## Findings

### A compression that destroyed the warning it was compressing

The most instructive defect this pass is one sentence in
[[Mykola-Bohdan Vynnytskyi - Understanding Databricks - Genie Explained]]. The summary's status line read
"GA for Genie One and Ontology **2026-06-16**".

The capture says the opposite, and says it emphatically: the Genie Ontology is "**Public Preview since
May 2026, despite what some coverage implies — it's not GA yet**". The author wrote that clause
specifically to stop readers from making this inference, because the Summit announcement was Genie One
going GA *alongside* the Ontology, not the Ontology going GA.

So the vault read a sentence whose entire purpose was to prevent a misreading, and then produced exactly
that misreading. Two details make this worth recording rather than just fixing. First, the summary
**contradicted itself two lines later**, correctly stating "Ontology is Public Preview since May 2026" —
the error and its refutation coexisted in one paragraph and survived ingest review. Second, the claim
wrapped across a line break, so `GA for\n  Genie One and Ontology` was invisible to every single-line
grep used during verification. The ingest's own checks could not have caught it; only reading the
summary against the capture did.

This is the second time this vault has been bitten by a line break. The October 23 ingest already
recorded that a wikilink split across a line does not resolve. The same wrapping also breaks
**verification**, not just linking.

### A number that migrated between bodies of evidence

[[Recursive Self-Improvement]] attributed Naam's **five to ten times below self-sustaining strength**
figure to "the measured parallel-scaling curves" assembled in
[[Ramez Naam - Can a Million AI Agents Create Superintelligence]].

That figure is not a result of this essay. It comes from Naam's **earlier** RSI post and calibrates the
**software feedback loop** — gains from smarter models. The multi-agent scaling curves in this essay are
separate third-party evidence, and Naam attaches **no comparable multiple** to them; he reports only
that multi-agent intelligence gains look logarithmic or sub-logarithmic.

The source summary had this right. The concept page acquired the error while merging the number into
surrounding prose, which is the more dangerous direction: a quantitative claim gained unearned support
from a body of evidence that never produced it.

### New pages that did not know what the vault already held

Two of the ten concepts created on October 23 were written only from their triggering source, while the
vault already contained substantive evidence they never reached. This is a structural consequence of
ingesting a batch: a new page is drafted from the new capture, and nothing in the workflow forces it to
search the existing corpus.

- **[[Semantic Caching]]** cited only the ByteByteGo comparison, yet
  [[Sarthak Rastogi - Making an AI Agent Production-Ready]] had been in the vault since July with the
  only *operational* account of a semantic cache: where the lookup sits relative to the agent graph, and
  why the cache runs as its own process.
- **[[Text-to-SQL Agents]]** cited only the two Databricks articles — both vendor-framed — while
  [[ByteByteGo - System Design and AI at Scale (May 2026 Batch)]] held the vault's only **production**
  deployment, Grab's agent over 15,000+ tables, including the pre-execution guardrail layers the
  Databricks material lacks entirely.

The second case mattered more than a missing link. The page asserted that "the vault's source supplies
no evaluation harness", which was true of its cited sources but obscured a real distinction: Grab's
layers block **dangerous and expensive** queries without establishing that a permitted query returns the
**right number**. Guardrails and correctness evaluation are different problems, and the page now says so.

### Affected-page reciprocity had drifted, invisibly, to 22 violations

The vault enforces a reciprocity invariant: a page listed under a summary's `## Affected pages` must
carry that summary's source ID, and every source ID on a page must appear in that summary's affected
list. Every recent ingest reports this as verified with zero failures.

Those verifications are **scoped to the batch being ingested**. Measured across the whole vault for the
first time this pass, the invariant held in the reverse direction but failed **22 times** forward — all
in pages predating the October 23 batch. The ingest-time check is not wrong, but it cannot detect drift
in material it does not look at, and nothing else was looking.

Measuring the same thing against the pre-lint baseline returned an identical 22, confirming the backlog
was pre-existing rather than introduced by the October 23 work.

The 22 split cleanly in two once each target page was checked for *where* it referenced the summary:

- **16** discussed the source in body prose but omitted its ID. Both sides agreed the source had
  contributed; only the frontmatter disagreed. These were under-citations.
- **6** referenced the summary **only** under `## Related pages`, with no prose citation anywhere. Under
  the rule fixed by the 2026-08-27 pass — affected pages are those an ingest actually changed and that
  will cite the ID — these were summaries **over-claiming** their reach.

A first attempt to measure this produced 48 spurious "unknown source ID" hits, all from collection
sources. That was a bug in the check, not the vault: batch summaries own a primary `source_id` **plus**
a `source_ids` list of per-item sub-IDs, which is why 316 summaries own 342 IDs. Worth recording, since
any future script that assumes one ID per summary will rediscover the same false positives.

### Verified clean

The remaining 23 chains traced clean across all six passes. Spot-confirmed as correctly hedged and
correctly attributed: Guan et al.'s exclusion of GPT-6 Astra and its 7 configurations / 500 bootstrap
replicates; Husain's 0.82 and 3.72 cents per 100 runs against one private 98-trace dataset, with
decision-focused learning credited to Wilder, Dilkina and Tambe (2018); the TorchTPU figures of 1,828
TPS PyTorch versus 1,937 TPS JAX/MaxText with PyTorch correctly trailing, and the 19.2 TBps / 19.2 Tb/s
unit conflict preserved as unresolved rather than silently reconciled; MemCurrent's 31% and 26% tied to
its internal no-memory baseline; the Uber Genie 13,000-hour derivation still correctly refused as
unrecoverable; and the Databricks 84.5% versus 52.4% accuracy claim still carrying its vendor label and
missing-methodology warning.

Two disambiguations held everywhere they appear: the two unrelated **Genie** systems, and the two
unrelated **"dreaming"** mechanisms.

## Fixes made

Twenty-one pages changed. No page was created or deleted, and no raw capture was touched.

- **Genie Ontology status corrected** in the Vynnytskyi summary. The line now reads "Genie One **GA
  2026-06-16**" and states that the Ontology is Public Preview since May 2026, carrying the author's
  explicit "not GA" warning and the *alongside* distinction into the summary itself.
- **Naam's five-to-ten-times figure reattributed** in [[Recursive Self-Improvement]] to his earlier
  software-feedback-loop calibration, with the parallel-scaling curves explicitly marked as separate
  evidence carrying no comparable multiple. No number was changed, added, or removed.
- **[[Semantic Caching]]** gained the Rastogi source and a section on cache placement — the check sits
  outside the agent graph because invoking the graph is not free, and the cache runs as a separate
  process because its embedding model is compute-heavy. The open question about miss cost now records
  that this is a partial answer: the overhead is treated as large enough to isolate, but **no hit rate
  or break-even figure was invented** to close it.
- **[[Text-to-SQL Agents]]** gained the ByteByteGo batch source and a section on Grab's pre-execution
  guardrails, and its failure-mode claim was narrowed to distinguish safety-and-cost validation from
  correctness evaluation. Grab's reported gains are labelled first-party and unablated.
- **Reciprocity cleared from 22 to 0.** Sixteen pages received the source ID they already discussed in
  prose. Six over-claims were removed from the affected lists of
  [[Sarthak Rastogi - Making an AI Agent Production-Ready]] and
  [[Alpha Signal - Why self-improving harnesses are the next frontier]]; every one of those six was
  already listed under the summary's own `## Related pages`, so no relationship was lost.

Single-source concepts fell from **14 to 12**. Control parity remains exact at **342** IDs across index,
log, and overview; none of the fixes introduced a new source ID, since every ID added was already
registered.

## Follow-ups

- **Reciprocity needs a standing vault-wide check.** The batch-scoped version reported zero while 22
  violations stood. Any future ingest verification should run the full-corpus check, and must treat
  `source_id` plus `source_ids` as the ownership set.
- **Line-wrapped claims defeat grep-based verification.** Both this pass and the October 23 ingest lost
  something to a line break. Verification scripts should normalize whitespace before matching.
- **New concept pages should be checked against the existing corpus before filing.** Two of ten missed
  evidence the vault already held. A keyword sweep of existing summaries at page-creation time would
  have caught both.
- **[[Medallion Architecture]] remains a legitimate single-source concept.** No other wiki page or raw
  capture mentions medallion or bronze/silver/gold layering. This is a genuine coverage gap, not a
  citation failure, and a second independent source would be worth seeking.
- **The data-platform cluster links outward but not inward.** The eight new Databricks-derived concepts
  reference older pages; almost no older page references them. They are reachable, so this is not a
  structural defect, but the asymmetry means the cluster is unlikely to be found by someone browsing
  from the vault's established areas.
- Standing buckets are unchanged and remain non-blocking: 24 legacy lint reports without empty
  `source_ids`, 34 legacy raw links under Citations, 8 captures with known-missing URLs, 1 reused
  chronological log heading, and 8 short entity pages (77–95 words) worth expanding.
