# Jacky Kwok et al - Contrastive Language Models

## Summary

A Stanford and NVIDIA Research team proposes Contrastive Language Models: separate state and action
encoders trained with bidirectional InfoNCE to place matched state-action pairs near each other in a
shared embedding space. At deployment, a decision is a similarity lookup over candidate actions, so
action embeddings can be precomputed and cached independently of the state. CLM-8B is reported as
comparable to Jev across computer-use, gaming, and tool-calling tasks at substantially lower latency.
This is the first entry in the vault's typed-decision thread that comes from an academic group rather
than the vendor whose product it benchmarks against.

## Key claims

- Architecture: a state encoder and an action encoder over frozen LLM backbones, scored by cosine
  similarity. Only a **20M-parameter projection head** is trained; LLM embeddings are precomputed and
  reused. A full pre-training run on Nemotron DQA takes **about an hour on a single RTX 4090 GPU**.
- Caching is the source of the speedup: candidate action embeddings are state-independent, so in the
  Super Mario example **4 action embeddings** are precomputed and cost falls **from 5 forward passes
  to just 1 per step**. At around **1k candidates**, CLM is reported **13x faster than Jev**.
- Training recipe: **~60M** Nemotron DQA question-answer pairs (pre-training), **~30M** synthetic hard
  negatives generated by **Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite** (mid-training), and **~1M** ADP agent trajectories
  supplemented by Endless-Terminals and LiteCoder-Terminal-SFT (post-training).
- The two-stage curriculum is reported as worth **7% higher accuracy at fixed budget**: pre-training
  alone reaches **52.1%** top-1, pre-training plus mid-training reaches **69.2%**, while hard
  negatives from scratch peak at **62.4%** before overfitting - evaluated on **~100K held-out
  questions**, each with one gold answer and **10 hard negatives**.
- Replay mixing of **40%** Nemotron DQA with **60%** agentic trajectories costs almost nothing on
  hard-negative top-1 (**69% to 68.5%**), whereas training on agentic data alone **for the same
  number of agentic steps** collapses it to **56.2%**.
- Reward-model results: **81.6% on DeepSWE** and **87.6% on Terminal-Bench 2.1**, selecting from
  candidate solutions sampled with **Opus 5** and **Fable 5** respectively, over **38** and **30**
  held-out tasks, latency on an **H100 GPU**.
- Scaling: `L(X) ~ (X_c/X)^alpha_X` over compute, dataset size, projection-head size, and encoder
  size, with encoder size reported as producing the strongest gains; optimal head size scales as
  `N* ~ D^1.02`, about **310 tokens per parameter**.

## Why it matters

The vault's typed-decision evidence has so far been vendor-shaped, with speed and cost claims
originating from TypeSafe AI and repeated downstream. CLM is an independent group building a
competing architecture and publishing Jev as its baseline, which is the first outside pressure on
those numbers. It also isolates *why* such systems are fast: not a smaller model, but a decision
formulated so the expensive side of the comparison can be computed once and reused. That is the same
structural move the vault records in retrieval and in cached prefixes, applied to action selection.

## Tensions and caveats

Every figure is first-party and self-reported; "SOTA" is the authors' own characterization, the venue
is a Notion page rather than a peer-reviewed paper, and the capture arrived with no author in its
frontmatter - the seven-author list was recovered from the body and its BibTeX entry at ingest. The
speed claims are condition-dependent and must not be merged: the page states **up to 9x lower
latency** overall,
**4-6x faster inference than Jev** in the benchmark section, and **13x** at roughly 1k candidates.
The DeepSWE and Terminal-Bench percentages rest on **38** and **30** tasks. The method presumes a
defined candidate action set and is not open-ended generation. Synthetic hard negatives inherit
Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite's artifacts. The scaling section gives functional forms without fitted
exponents, and improvements in contrastive test loss do not by themselves establish verification
reliability. The robotics reference to CoVer-VLA is a forward pointer, not evidence presented here.
