# 2026-09-04 Lint Pass

Thirty-second comprehensive lint pass, run one day after the five-source September 4 ingest so it doubles as a
post-ingest audit of that batch. Scope: **406 wiki pages, 229 raw captures, 222 registered source IDs.**

All ten mechanical checks pass after the fixes below. The defect that mattered was invisible to every one of
them.

## Headline: a claim that got stronger as it travelled

[[Can Bölük - The Harness Playbook]] reports a wall-clock experiment on task `sol`. The raw capture says it
plainly: the author received a complaint that his harness *omp* was slower than Codex, expected "a
nothing-burger," and found it true — **"almost two times even."** He then writes: *"The culprit was the tool
roster. Limit it to five essential tools and you get `36.6s`, ahead of Codex's `42.2s` and Pi's `37.0s`."* The
chart caption marks omp variants in cyan and Codex and Pi as **grey external references**.

So the structure of the evidence is: **one harness measured before and after a roster change**, with two other
harnesses shown at their own default rosters as reference points.

The source summary recorded this correctly. Then six derived pages restated it as:

> **36.6s for five essential tools against 42.2s for a full-roster harness on the same task.**

That sentence describes a different experiment — one harness swept across two roster sizes. It is cleaner, it is
more quotable, and it is not what was measured. Nothing in it is a wrong number; the numbers are identical on
every page. What changed is **which comparison the numbers are claimed to support**, and the drifted version
credits the roster with a 5.6-second cross-harness gap that is confounded with two entirely different
implementations.

Worse, the compression **discarded the strong result**. The within-harness before/after — from almost twice
Codex's wall clock down to 36.6s — is the controlled comparison, the one that actually isolates the roster, and
the phrase "almost two times" appeared **nowhere in the vault**. The ingest kept the weak evidence, dropped the
strong evidence, and phrased the weak evidence as though it were the strong one.

**No check could have caught this.** Every link resolved (check 2). Reciprocity held (check 5). Ordering,
orphans, and parity were clean. The owner page, [[Tool Roster Economics]], carried the confound explicitly in its
own `## Open questions` — *"the comparison is across different harnesses on one task, so roster is confounded with
implementation"* — while five pages downstream of it stated the unqualified version. **A caveat on the owner page
does not travel with the number.**

## The general form of this defect

The vault already has a written defence against exactly this failure — in the **post** workflow, which requires a
compression check before a draft is marked ready, on the reasoning that *shortening is where honest claims
quietly become false ones*. That rule is scoped to social posts. This pass is evidence the same failure mode
operates **inside the wiki**, during ingest, where no such check exists.

The mechanism is worth stating precisely, because it is not carelessness:

- A source's finding arrives with its conditions attached.
- The source summary records the conditions, because summarising is its job.
- A concept page needs one sentence, not a paragraph, so the conditions are dropped.
- Three more pages copy the concept page's sentence, because it is the sentence that reads well.
- The conditions now exist in one place, and the claim exists in six.

The vault ingested Bölük's own prescription for this on the same day — *"one owner for each fact, explicit
precedence, and an `unknown` state"* — and then violated it about his own benchmark. Facts have an owner here;
what they lack is **precedence**, a way for a derived page to inherit the owner's qualifications instead of
paraphrasing past them.

**Recorded as a schema question, not fixed:** should a page restating another page's headline number be required
to either carry the qualifier or say "see X for conditions"? That is a rule the schema could state and a future
check could approximate, but designing it is the schema owner's call.

### Figure propagation, measured

How many pages carry each of the September 4 batch's headline facts:

| Fact | Pages |
|---|---|
| 36.6s / 42.2s roster timings | 7 |
| "2 of 17 stateful examples correct" | 6 |

| 78-example audit | 6 |
| "saved tokens locally and spent more globally" | 6 |
| ~80% fewer tokens per turn | 4 |
| 22-layer stack twice, ~75% token efficiency | 4 |

Repetition is by design — concept pages must stand alone. The finding is not that facts repeat. It is that
**qualifiers do not repeat with them**, and the ratio above is the blast radius of any single misstatement.

Verified: apart from the roster timings, every repeated figure is stated **consistently** across all its pages.
No other numeric drift was found.

## Second fix: the check-10 candidate was a real page defect

Check 10 (dangling reference) returned two candidates, both pointing at the same target: [[SIMD]], linked from
body prose by [[Software Performance Engineering]] and [[Jeff Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat - Performance Hints]].

Both pointers were **sound** — the referring sentences claimed SIMD sits inside an optimization hierarchy, and
the page did say that. But the page itself was 214 words carrying 2 sources, with **no `## Definition`, no
`## Why it matters`, and no `## Open questions`**; its lede was bare prose before `## Current synthesis`, and its
frontmatter used inline-list tags. Rebuilt to **520 words** with the canonical spine, the fivefold-speedup figure
attributed and marked as a single case rather than an expected rate, the ceiling-versus-expectation framing made
explicit, and three open questions added. Check 10 now passes.

This is the second consecutive pass in which check 10 produced **a reading list rather than a verdict** — 5
candidates and 1 true defect on 2026-09-03, 2 candidates and 0 dangling pointers today, though today's did
surface a genuinely malformed page by a different route. The check earns its place by pointing at thin pages that
other pages depend on; it should not be read as an accusation.

This is an accretion pattern, not neglect. A page that gains a themed section from each of fifteen ingests ends
up with fifteen good sections and no spine, because every ingest adds and none reorganises. These pages are
individually excellent and collectively hard to enter — a reader arriving at 3,814 words with no definition has
to read to find out what the page is about. **Worth a dedicated restructuring pass; too large to fold into a
lint.**

**103 pages use inline `tags: [a, b, c]`** where the schema specifies a block list. This is the same shape as the
finding recorded on 2026-09-03 about the raw-capture pointer having two live encodings: a quarter of the vault
follows one convention and three quarters follow another, both parse, and nothing downstream cares. Recorded, not
churned.

a `- Raw capture:` bullet only, 89 use both, 0 use neither.** The template sanctions only the bullet form, and

"every summary points at its capture" holds vault-wide. Notably all five September 4 summaries used the

**28 of 204 summaries have alias-aware section gaps** (18 lack `## Key claims`, 18 lack `## Why it matters`),
